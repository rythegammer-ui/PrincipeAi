## 1. How we film (one-pager for a solo founder with a phone)

### 1.1 Kit

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| The phone you already have | Main camera and screen recorder | Clear 10 GB of storage before each batch |
| Phone tripod with a clamp | Steady, repeatable framing | Mark the floor spot and tripod height with tape so every week matches |
| Clip-on lavalier mic (wired or wireless) | Voice is the content | Clip it 15–20 cm (6–8 in) below your chin, away from clothing that rustles |
| A window, or one LED panel | Soft, even light on your face | See 1.3 |
| A laptop with the AI assistant open | Desktop screen recordings, and a teleprompter | Free teleprompter apps work, or read one line, look up, say it |
| Free editor: Edits (Meta), CapCut desktop or Descript | Captions, jump cuts, zooms | Export with **no** watermark or end card (see 1.7) |

### 1.2 Camera setup

- **Vertical 9:16, 1080 × 1920, 30 fps.** Lock exposure and focus on your face (long-press on most phones).
- **Use the rear camera if you can** (better quality; check the framing with a 5-second test clip, or a small mirror behind the phone). The front camera is fine if it means you'll actually film.
- **Lens at eye level, about an arm's length away.** Frame from mid-chest up, with your eyes on the line one-third from the top.
- **Safe zones:** keep your face and any text out of the top ~15% and bottom ~25% of the frame (app buttons and captions sit there) and away from the right edge (like and share buttons). Instagram's grid crops Reels to 4:5, so keep the key words in the middle of the frame.
- **Background:** uncluttered, a little depth behind you, nothing private on the walls or screens. A touch of royal violet or gold (a mug, a cushion, a print) ties the frame to the brand.

### 1.3 Lighting

- **Daytime:** face a window, so the light falls on your face rather than coming from behind you. Overcast daylight is the most flattering.
- **Evening:** one LED panel or ring light at 45° to one side, slightly above eye level. Turn off overhead lights so the colors don't mix.
- **Check:** both eyes have a small catch-light, and no harsh shadow falls under your nose or chin. Film every batch at the same time of day so the videos match.

### 1.4 Sound

- Pick the quietest room with soft furnishings (rugs, curtains, a sofa). Turn off fans, the fridge hum where possible, and notifications.
- Record 5 seconds of silence ("room tone") at the start of each batch; editors use it to smooth cuts.
- **Music:** your voice comes first. On TikTok use the Commercial Music Library only. On Shorts over 60 seconds use the YouTube Audio Library or no music, because any Content ID claim blocks a Short longer than 1 minute. Keep any track 15–20 dB under your voice.

### 1.5 Screen-recording an AI assistant

Every demo is a **real** screen recording of a real AI assistant answering a real prompt. That is what keeps these videos original on every platform, and it needs no AI label (see 2.6).

1. **Use a clean account.** A fresh chat, no sidebar history that shows anything personal, a neutral profile picture, and your email address off screen.
2. **Phone recording is easiest for vertical video.** Record the AI app with the phone's built-in screen recorder (Do Not Disturb on). For desktop, zoom the browser to 125–150% and record only the chat column, so you can crop it to 9:16 without unreadable text.
3. **Prepare the prompt first.** Copy it from the script block below into your notes app, fill in the brackets with **made-up or anonymized** details, then paste it in one go. Never type real client names, addresses, account numbers or private messages.
4. **Leave the prompt on screen for 1.5–2 seconds** so viewers can screenshot it, then show the answer arriving.
5. **Record each demo twice.** AI answers vary between runs; keep the better take. Trim and zoom freely, but never alter or fake the answer's text.
6. **Trim the waiting, and say so if you speed it up.** Jump-cut the thinking time. If you speed up the answer, add on-screen text such as "sped up 2×".
7. **Zoom in during the edit** on the part of the answer you're talking about. UI text is unreadable at phone size.
8. **Tool names and logos.** Say "AI assistants like Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini" when you name tools. It's fine for the real app interface to appear in a screen recording, but don't put their logos in thumbnails, cover images or title cards, and never say or imply "partner", "official", "certified" or "approved by".

### 1.6 Captions and on-screen text

- **Burn captions into every video.** Many people watch muted at first. Use the editor's auto-captions, then **proofread** every line; auto-captions often mishear "Claude" as "cloud" and garble "Gemini".
- **Caption style:** 2–4 words per line, large, high contrast, in the middle third of the frame. Use Geist (or the editor's closest clean sans-serif) in white on an ink `#0c0a1d` box, with the key word in gold `#fec84b`.
- **Hook title (first 2 seconds):** the exact on-screen text from each script, top-middle and clear of the app UI, in royal violet `#6938ef` with white text. Use Instrument Serif italic for one accent word if your editor supports custom fonts.
- **End card (last 2–3 seconds):** the CTA text from each script. Don't add a separate 5-second outro; end on the CTA so the loop restarts cleanly.

### 1.7 Export and upload

- **One clean master per video:** 1080 × 1920, 30 fps, H.264, **no** platform watermark, **no** CapCut template end clip. File name: `SV-03_master_v1.mp4`.
- **Two CTA endings.** Record the universal ending ("…link's in my profile") and a TikTok ending ("DM me KIT and I'll send it over"). Export a second file with the TikTok ending until your TikTok bio link is live (it needs a Verified Business Account or 1,000 followers).
- **Upload natively to each platform.** Never download a finished post from one platform to re-upload it on another, and never upload the same file twice to the same platform (re-cut it with a new hook instead).

### 1.8 The 2-hour filming batch (3 or 4 videos)

The calendar sets every batch and names the videos: batches A, B and C in Setup (L-17 · Sat Oct 24, L-10 · Sat Oct 31, L-7 · Tue Nov 3), then the Monday batch session from L+6 (Mon 9:30 to 11:30), which films 3 videos a week. Each block below names its batch in the **Film** row. The timings are for 4 videos; with 3, each step is a little shorter.

| Time | Do this |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | **Set up.** Light, tripod at the taped spot, mic on, phone on Do Not Disturb, storage clear. Fill in the brackets of every prompt in the batch in your notes app. Record 5 s of room tone and a 10-second test clip, then play it back with headphones |
| 0:15–0:50 | **All on-camera takes, back to back.** For each script: 3 versions of the hook (vary the first words), then each [ON CAMERA] beat, then both CTA endings. Read a line, look up, say it. Clap once between takes so they're easy to find |
| 0:50–1:30 | **All screen recordings.** One fresh chat per demo, two runs each. Hold every prompt on screen 2 seconds. Record any [B-ROLL] (scrolling a page, the assessment with a test address) |
| 1:30–1:45 | **Safety pass.** Scrub every recording for names, emails, notifications, open tabs and account details. Delete anything risky now |
| 1:45–2:00 | **File and log.** Rename clips by ID (`SV-07_hook_take2`), back them up, and note in your tracker which hook take you'll use first |

**The rest of the week** is in the calendar's time budget (section 2): about 20 minutes to edit each video on Tuesday and Wednesday, and one Thursday sitting to schedule the week. A standard Growth week posts 3 short videos on 4 platforms.

### 1.9 No-face version (any script)

Every script works without your face on camera. Pick one style per batch so the videos match.

- **Voice-over:** replace each **[ON CAMERA]** beat with the screen recording or B-roll the script already uses (the page, the AI chat, your hands at the desk), and record the same lines as a voice-over with the lav mic.
- **On-screen text only:** no face and no voice. Put each spoken line on screen as large captions, 2 to 4 words at a time, over the screen recording. Hold each line long enough to read it twice. Add a track from TikTok's Commercial Music Library or the YouTube Audio Library, kept quiet.
- **Keep the first 2 seconds moving:** open on the prompt being pasted, a zoom into the answer, or the hook text appearing.
- **The words stay the same.** In SV-01, keep "I'm [YOUR NAME]" in a voice-over; in a text-only cut you can open with "This is Principe AI" instead.
- Each block's **No-face option** line says what to show.

## 2. Links, CTAs, keywords and limits (rules every script follows)

### 2.1 The per-post link

Every link follows the final convention in [tools, measurement and compliance](08-tools-measurement-compliance.md), section 2. For a short video it looks like this:

```text
https://principeai.com{PATH}?utm_source={tiktok|instagram|youtube|facebook}&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign={launch|evergreen|ny-2027}&utm_content={post ID in lower case}
```

- **`utm_source`** is the platform the link sits on, so the Facebook copy of a Reel uses `facebook`.
- **`utm_campaign`** follows the day the video posts, as in the calendar: `launch` from L-7 to L+13 (Tue Nov 3 to Mon Nov 23), `evergreen` from L+14 to L+52 (Tue Nov 24 to Fri Jan 1), `ny-2027` from Sat Jan 2 to Sun Jan 31. Every block's links already carry the right value for its calendar date. If you move a video, change only the campaign.
- **New Year re-cuts** (SV-02 to SV-08) keep their IDs. Their own `ny-2027` links are at the end of each of those blocks.
- **No video here is a Black Friday promotion**, so none uses `bf-2026`. SV-16 posts on Black Friday weekend as a value video. The OPTIONAL promotion posts (VC-72 and TP-16 version A) need a real Stripe promotion code with a real end date, and never a "was" price.
