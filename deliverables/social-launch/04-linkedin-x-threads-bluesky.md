## How to use this kit

**What's in it**

1. **Launch week** (TP-01 to TP-08). The announcement, written separately for the LinkedIn profile, the LinkedIn Company Page, X (a single post and an 8-post thread), Threads and Bluesky, plus two follow-ups.
2. **LinkedIn library** (TP-09 to TP-38). 30 posts, including 3 document-carousel outlines, a founder-story template and three polls. The calendar schedules all of them except TP-35, which waits in its Reserve. TP-34 and TP-29 are your warm-up posts, and TP-32 and TP-33 go on the Company Page only.
3. **Short posts for X, Threads and Bluesky** (TP-39 to TP-62). 24 prompt tips, myths, seasonal posts and mini-threads, each cut to fit all three platforms.
4. **A daily 20-minute engagement routine** (section 4).
5. **A LinkedIn newsletter plan**, with name ideas and the first four issues outlined (TP-63 to TP-66). Issue 1 goes out on L+56 · Tue Jan 5.
6. **Every post's date** in one table (section 7), copied from the calendar.

**How each post is laid out.** A heading with the ID, platform and topic. Then a short list: when to post (from the calendar), the format, where the link goes, the tagged link, anything you must fill in, and the source in the site's code. Then the post itself in a grey box: copy the box as it is. The italic line under each box is its length against that platform's limit. Tags such as [L16] or [X9] point to the source list in section 9 of the platform research behind this kit, and "research §3.1" to a section of it ([research/text-professional.md](research/text-professional.md) in this folder). You don't need it to post.

**Two kinds of brackets.** Inside a quoted prompt, `[CAPITALS IN BRACKETS]` belong to the prompt. Leave them in, because your readers fill them in. Placeholders for you are listed under **Fill in first** on each post. They are `[YOUR NAME]`, `[YOUR STORY: …]`, `[YOUR BACKGROUND: …]`, `[YOUR CREDENTIAL IF ANY]`, `[YOUR OBSERVATION: …]`, `[YOUR EXAMPLE: …]`, `[YOUR OFFER: …]`, `[END DATE AND TIME, ET]`, `[OPTIONAL: …]` and `[CONFIRM: AI key live in production]`. Use only true details. If you have nothing true to put in one, delete the whole line. Before posting, search the post for `[YOUR NAME`, `[YOUR STORY`, `[YOUR BACKGROUND`, `[YOUR CREDENTIAL`, `[YOUR OBSERVATION`, `[YOUR EXAMPLE`, `[YOUR OFFER`, `[END DATE`, `[OPTIONAL:` and `[CONFIRM`. No prompt placeholder starts with any of those, so anything the search finds is yours to fill in.

**Claims to hold back until they're true** (the same rules as the [content bank](07-content-bank.md), section 6)

- **"AI tutor inside every lesson" and "a plan written by Claude"** only with a `[CONFIRM: AI key live in production]` tag, because the site falls back to plain text without `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`. In this kit only TP-16 version A has such a line. Keep it only if the key is live (calendar checkpoint L-20 · Wed Oct 21); otherwise delete the line.
- **The downloadable course app and the PDF course book** are sent by hand today and aren't on the course pages. No post mentions them. Add them only once delivery is automatic and the course pages list them.
- **Never say:** testimonials, student counts, ratings, "trusted by", time-saved or revenue figures, "money-back guarantee", guaranteed results, or a struck-through or "was" price. Terms §12: "We don't guarantee any particular outcome, such as time saved, revenue or business results."
- **AI tools** are named only as "AI assistants like Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini", in plain text, with no logos and no hint of partnership ([tools, measurement and compliance](08-tools-measurement-compliance.md), 4.9). In About-style text, here the newsletter description in 5.2, add: "Principe AI is independent and not affiliated with Anthropic, OpenAI or Google."
- **Black Friday:** TP-16 version A is **OPTIONAL** and needs a real Stripe promotion code with a real end date. Decide at the calendar's go/no-go on L+10 · Fri Nov 20. Without a code, post version B.
- **Your story:** never invent one. Put only true details in the `[YOUR …]` placeholders, or delete the line. Every post that would show you has a no-face option on its **Format** line.

## 0. Ground rules this kit follows

### 0.1 Links and UTM tags

Every link follows the final convention in [tools, measurement and compliance](08-tools-measurement-compliance.md), section 2. A script wrote each one, so copy them; never retype a link.

| Link | Pattern |
|---|---|
| **Post link** (in a post, your first reply, a Threads text attachment or the newsletter) | `https://principeai.com{PATH}?utm_source={linkedin, x, threads or bluesky}&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign={campaign}&utm_content=tp-NN` |
| **DM link** (a message you send by hand to someone who asked) | `https://principeai.com{PATH}?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=dm&utm_campaign=evergreen&utm_content={dm-kit, dm-ready, dm-workflow or tp-NN}` |

- **`utm_source`**: `linkedin`, `x`, `threads` or `bluesky`, never a variant. Admin → Funnels groups leads on this field. The LinkedIn newsletter is `linkedin` too.
- **`utm_campaign`** follows the day the post goes out, as in the calendar: `launch` from L-7 to L+13 (Tue Nov 3 to Mon Nov 23), `evergreen` from L+14 (Tue Nov 24) to Fri Jan 1 and again from February, `ny-2027` from Jan 2 to 31, and `bf-2026` only for TP-16 version A with a real promotion code. DM links are always `evergreen`. If you move a post into another phase, change only this value.
- **`utm_content`**: the post ID in lowercase, such as `tp-09`. Short posts use the same ID on all three platforms, and `utm_source` tells them apart. DM links use the keyword values whichever post prompted them: `dm-kit` for the Starter Kit (`/free-guide`), `dm-ready` for the assessment (`/assessment`) and `dm-workflow` for the free lesson Anatomy of an AI Workflow. A DM to any other page keeps the post ID, because the convention has no DM value for it yet.
- **Where you'll see them:** the platform shows in Admin → Funnels → Attribution → "Channel (UTM source)". The campaign is in the contacts CSV export. The post ID (or the `dm-` value) shows only on each contact's own page ([content bank](07-content-bank.md), 2b).
- **Don't** link or tag `/guide`, `/free-guide/thank-you`, `/assessment/results/…`, `/booking/…` or `/book/confirmed/…`. Starter Kit links always go to `/free-guide`. **Do** open every link in a private window once before you use it ([tools, measurement and compliance](08-tools-measurement-compliance.md), 2.8).

### 0.2 Where the link goes on each platform

| Platform | Default | Exceptions in this kit | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn profile | **No link in the post.** Point to your Featured section, or offer to send it. Send the post's **DM link** to anyone who asks. | At most 1 post in 5 carries the link in the body: TP-01 (launch), TP-16 (Black Friday), TP-37 and TP-38 (offers). | Third-party studies put the reach cut for links at 45-70%. First-comment links aren't reliable any more either [L16][L24]. |
| LinkedIn Company Page | Link in the body. | None. | Page posts are announcements, and Page reach is weak either way [L22]. |
| X | The value goes in the post. The link goes in **your own first reply**. | Threads: the link goes in the last post. | X says links aren't penalized, but link posts still have the lowest engagement [X7][X9][B1]. |
| Threads | The link goes in the **text attachment**. | Offer posts (TP-05, TP-08) and mini-threads: the link goes in the post. | Meta says links are "ranked properly". Link posts are still slightly behind text posts [T7][B1]. |
| Bluesky | The link goes **in the post**. | None. | Link posts did as well as text posts [B1]. |

**Keyword CTAs** ("comment KIT"): at most one a week on LinkedIn, and only to deliver something people asked for. LinkedIn says it is filtering engagement bait [L14]. In this kit only TP-10 uses one. The other posts say "message me" or "it's in my Featured section".

**Hashtags:** 0-3 on LinkedIn, none on X, one topic tag on Threads (choose it in the composer), and 1-2 on Bluesky [L20][X11][T1][BS10].

### 0.3 Limits used to check every post

| | LinkedIn | X (free account) | Threads | Bluesky |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Post | 3,000 characters [L1] | 280, weighted [X1] | 500, plus a 10,000-character text attachment [T3] | 300 graphemes [BS9] |
| Polls | Question 140, options 30 [L10] | Not used | Not used | No native polls |

How the counts were worked out:
- **X:** most letters and punctuation count as 1. Characters such as "→" and "…" count as 2. Every link counts as 23. That is X's standard counting method; the research didn't re-check it, so each count also gives the total with the link at full length. All X posts fit the free 280 limit, so they work with or without Premium (25,000 characters [X1]).
- **Bluesky:** counts **include the full link**, so every post fits even if Bluesky counts the whole URL. The research couldn't verify how links are counted [BS9]. If you ever need the room: paste the link, wait for the link card to appear, then delete the link text. This is the research's hypothesis, so check it once before relying on it.
- **Placeholders** are counted as written, so a long name adds characters.

```text linkedin
"Agent" is the most overused word in AI right now. Strip away the hype and the idea is simple:

An agent is a model that's given a goal and a set of tools, and works in a loop, deciding what to do next, doing it and looking at the result, until the goal is met or it needs help.

In a workflow, you decide the steps in advance. In an agent, the model decides them as it goes. That buys flexibility, and it costs predictability, money and speed.

Before you build one, ask four questions:
1. Is the task genuinely multi-step and hard to spell out in advance? If you can draw the flowchart, build the workflow.
2. Is the outcome valuable enough to justify more cost, time and oversight?
3. Can AI do this kind of task well? Test it by hand in a chat assistant first.
4. Can mistakes be caught and fixed before they cause harm?

A "no" to any of these means a workflow or a single AI step is the better choice.

And even the simplest agent needs limits, starting with a cap on steps.
```

## 4. Daily 20-minute engagement routine

At zero followers, your comments under other people's posts will be seen by more people than your own posts. The research's version takes 25-30 minutes [L15]. This one fits in 20 by using a saved list and a fixed order. It is the calendar's daily engagement block (section 3.1 of the [calendar](02-launch-calendar.md)): every weekday, starting when your main post of the day goes live (14:30 ET on LinkedIn days). LinkedIn commenting starts on L-15 · Mon Oct 26 (10 minutes a weekday), and the full block starts on L-6 · Wed Nov 4. Skip it on the no-post days (Tue Nov 3, Thu Nov 26, Dec 24 and 25, Dec 31 and Jan 1).

Replies on your own posts come first in the block. On LinkedIn, stay for 30 minutes after a post goes live when you can; on Threads and X, reply within the first hour [L15][B2].

### 4.1 The 20 minutes

| Minutes | Platform | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| 0-4 | All | Reply to every new comment and DM on your own posts, Bluesky included. Accept connection requests from real people in your two lanes. Answer KIT, READY and WORKFLOW messages with the DM links in [profile setup](01-profile-setup.md), section 8. |
| 4-12 | LinkedIn | Leave **3 or 4 substantive comments**, of 2-4 sentences each, with one concrete tip (formula in 4.3). Make 2 of them on accounts from your saved list and the rest in your 2nd- and 3rd-degree network. Stay inside your two lanes: commenting off-topic confuses LinkedIn about what you're about [L15]. Then send **5 connection requests** to people whose posts you just commented on, or to owners and professionals in your lanes. Work up to 10 a day once it's quick [L15]. A free account can add a note to only 3 to 5 requests a month (LinkedIn Help, see 4.4), so save the note templates in 4.4 for your most valuable requests and send the rest without a note. |
| 12-18 | Community slot | The calendar rotates this slot by day. **Fridays are Threads and X:** reply under 2 Threads posts in AI tips, small business or productivity, and under 2-3 posts from mid-size X accounts (5k-100k followers) in AI for business, small business and productivity [research §3.6]. Every reply should add something, because X scores reply quality, and accounts under 1,000 followers get an extra spam check [X9]. The other days (Instagram and TikTok, Facebook groups, Reddit) are in the calendar. |
| 18-20 | Your log | One line: the date, comments, requests sent and any conversation worth following up. |

### 4.2 Who to comment on

Build a saved list of about 30 accounts in week one. Refresh it every Monday (section 4.6). Use five kinds of account:

1. **Peers who teach practical AI** to small businesses, professionals or beginners. Pick ones who show real prompts and real limits rather than hype. A thoughtful comment from a peer is seen by exactly your audience.
2. **Small-business voices:**
   - small-business coaches
   - bookkeepers and accountants
   - marketing consultants who serve small businesses
   - operations and productivity writers

   Their followers are the buyers of AI for Business Owners and the implementation service.
3. **Local and industry institutions:**
   - your local chambers of commerce
   - Small Business Development Centers (SBDCs)
   - SCORE chapters
   - industry and trade associations in the sectors the course names: "service businesses, trades, agencies, consultancies, e-commerce, local businesses and professional practices" (`course-ai-for-business.ts` FAQ)

   Comment as a helpful local expert, never with a pitch.
4. **Owners and managers in your lanes:** people posting about their own business problems (follow-up, admin, hiring, content). They're prospects, so comment to help, then connect.
5. **Life-lane voices:** careers, parenting and education, and personal productivity creators who post about using AI day to day.

**How to find them:**
- **LinkedIn:** search posts, sorted by latest, for phrases like "small business" AI, "using ChatGPT" OR "using Claude" for work, or "AI for my business". Follow the people who comment well under those posts too.
- **Threads:** browse the "AI Tips" and "Small Business" topic tags.
- **X:** search for the same phrases and filter by "Latest".
- **Bluesky:** follow AI, small-business and productivity custom feeds and starter packs. Once you've posted for a few weeks, ask curators to add you [BS4].

**Skip:**
- viral hot-takes outside your lanes
- news about AI company drama
- anything where the honest comment would be "great post"
- engagement pods, automation tools and AI-written comments. Van der Blom reports that most of the comments he gets in the first five minutes are AI-written, and commenting as a human is what will set you apart [L15].

### 4.3 How to comment (add, show, ask)

A useful comment has three parts, in 2-4 sentences:

1. **Add** one thing the post didn't say.
2. **Show** it with one concrete tip or line from your own material.
3. **Ask** one real question back.

Write each comment fresh for the post in front of you. These starters show the shape, and each tip comes from the repo:

| When the post is about… | A tip you can add | Repo source |
|---|---|---|
| AI giving generic answers | "Context is the most powerful part, and the one most often skipped." Suggest letting the AI ask up to five questions before it answers. | `the-five-part-prompt` |
| AI making things up in business docs | The [CHECK] rule: "If something is missing or unclear, write [CHECK] instead of guessing." | `business-prompt-framework` |
| Fear of AI replacing judgement | "AI drafts, humans decide," and lighten the review only "as evidence builds that a workflow is reliable". | `where-ai-pays-off` |
| Overloaded weeks and planning | Ask the AI to "tell me honestly what won't fit, and what I could drop, delay or delegate." | `starter-kit.ts` → "Plan my week" |
| Data privacy worries | The Green / Amber / Red rule, or the postcard rule. | `business-context-safely`, `set-up-your-ai-assistant` |
| "We should automate X" | "If 'amount is over $500' decides the route, use a filter, not a model." | `anatomy-of-an-ai-workflow` |
| A team resisting AI | "Adoption is a people challenge; address fears honestly before teaching tools." | `train-your-team` |

Example shape, under a post about slow lead follow-up:

```text
Agree on speed. One thing that helps: have AI draft the follow-up but make it reference one specific thing the person said, because a generic "just checking in" is noise. Do you send the first follow-up the same day, or after a couple of days?
```

Never put a link in a comment unless someone asks for it. Never mention your course in someone else's comments.

### 4.5 Rules that protect the account

- **Never** use engagement pods, auto-commenters, auto-connect tools, bought followers or bought engagement. - **Always** stay in the two lanes on LinkedIn, so the ranking system learns what Principe AI is about [L14][L15].
- On X, follow back real people in your niche; mutual follows get a visibility boost [X10].
