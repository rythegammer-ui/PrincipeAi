# Lesson 3: Set Up Your AI Assistant (and Protect Your Privacy)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Course** | AI Essentials: Use AI in Everyday Life |
| **Module** | 1 · Getting Started |
| **Length** | 4–5 min (~670 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Choose an AI assistant, set it up safely, check the four privacy settings that matter, and know what never to paste into a chat.

## Before you record

- Log in to your assistant on its official website or app and open a new chat. Keep your password, email address and account details off screen.
- Have the first-conversation prompt from the written lesson ready, and decide what you'll put in place of each bracketed part: your role or situation, two or three things you'd like help with, and how you like your answers.
- Ask your editor to prepare the "never paste" list from the written lesson as a simple title card.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Set Up Your AI Assistant (and Protect Your Privacy)"]**

Choosing an AI assistant can feel like choosing a phone plan: lots of options, lots of opinions. Here's the reassuring truth. For everyday use the major assistants are all capable, and every skill in this course works with any of them.

The best assistant is the one you'll actually open. So let's get you set up properly, in about ten minutes.

### 2. Pick one assistant · 0:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Claude · ChatGPT · Gemini · Microsoft Copilot"]**

The most widely used general-purpose assistants include Claude, made by Anthropic, ChatGPT from OpenAI, Gemini from Google, and Microsoft Copilot. Most offer a free version that's plenty for this course, plus paid plans with higher limits and extra features.

If you already live in Google or Microsoft tools, their assistants may fit neatly into your day. If you're using AI for work, check whether your employer provides or approves a specific tool. Features change often, so don't overthink it: pick one now, and add a second anytime.

### 3. Set it up safely · 1:05

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Official app · Strong password · Two-step verification"]**

Use the official website or official app, and in an app store, check the developer name carefully. Copycat apps exist, and some charge for things that are free. Then use a strong, unique password, and turn on two-step verification if it's offered.

Install the app on your phone too. Many of the most useful moments happen away from your desk: a quick message, a photo of a confusing form, a question while you're cooking.

### 4. Four privacy settings · 1:35

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Training · History · Memory · Temporary chats"]**

Next, open your assistant's settings and look for four things. Names vary by product, but the ideas are the same. First, model training or data controls: decide whether your conversations can be used to improve their models, and set it on purpose.

Second, chat history: know where your past conversations live and how to delete one. Third, memory or personalization: find where to view, edit or switch it off. Fourth, temporary or private chats, which some assistants offer for one-off sensitive questions.

Policies differ between companies and plans, and they change over time. Five minutes on your assistant's privacy page is time well spent.

### 5. The postcard rule · 2:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "The postcard rule: write as if someone else could read it"]**

Now the most important habit in this lesson: the postcard rule. Write as if your message could be read by someone else. Not because anyone is likely to read it, but because it keeps you careful by default.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Never paste: passwords and codes · card, bank or ID numbers · other people's private information · unapproved confidential work information"]**

So never paste passwords, PINs or security codes, or full card, bank account or government ID numbers. Don't share other people's private information without a good reason, or confidential work information your employer hasn't approved for AI tools.

You can still get help with sensitive topics. Just remove or swap the identifying details. "My client, a small-business owner in Ohio" works just as well as their name and address.

### 6. Your first real conversation · 3:05

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Hi! I'm setting you up as my everyday AI assistant. A little about me:
- I'm a [YOUR ROLE OR SITUATION, E.G. "TEACHER AND PARENT OF TWO"]
- I'd love help with [2-3 THINGS, E.G. "EMAILS, MEAL PLANNING, SPANISH"]
- I prefer answers that are [E.G. "SHORT AND PRACTICAL, WITH BULLETS"]

Based on this, suggest five specific ways you could help me this week.
Keep each suggestion to one sentence.
~~~

Here's a first real conversation. I'm swapping each bracketed part for my own details before I send it. Notice there's nothing sensitive in here, just enough for the assistant to be useful.

You'll get five specific suggestions, each one sentence long. Pick one and try it today.

### 7. Common mistakes · 3:40

**[ON CAMERA]**

A few common mistakes. Downloading the first app with "AI" in its name, instead of checking the developer. Skipping the settings, when five minutes now saves you wondering later where your data went.

Pasting first, thinking second: skim any document for personal or confidential details before you paste it. And trying five tools at once: get comfortable with one first, because the skills transfer.

### 8. Try it now · 4:10

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Try it now"]**

Your turn. Create your account or log in using the official site or app, then find those four privacy settings and set each one deliberately. Send the first-conversation prompt from the written lesson and try one of its suggestions.

Bonus: install the mobile app and ask one question from your phone.

### 9. Recap and what's next · 4:30

**[ON CAMERA]**

Quick recap. The major assistants are all capable, so pick one and start. Use the official app or website, a strong password and two-step verification.

Review your privacy settings on day one, and follow the postcard rule: never share passwords, financial or ID numbers, or confidential data.
