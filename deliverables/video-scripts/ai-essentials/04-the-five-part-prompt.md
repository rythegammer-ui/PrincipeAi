# Lesson 4: The 5-Part Prompt: Role, Context, Task, Format, Examples

**Goal of this video:** Write prompts using role, context, task, format and examples, so you get a useful answer the first time instead of a generic one.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat, and be ready to open a second new chat for the "after" prompt.
- Have both neighbor prompts from the written lesson's "Before and after" section ready to paste.
- Ask your editor to prepare the five parts (Role, Context, Task, Format, Examples) as a title card that can build up one word at a time.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "The 5-Part Prompt"]**

Two people ask the same AI assistant for help with the same problem. One gets a bland, generic answer and decides AI is overhyped. The other gets something so useful they use it almost word for word.

The difference isn't luck or a paid plan. It's the prompt, which is simply what you type to the AI. And nearly every great prompt contains some mix of five parts.

### 2. The five parts · 0:30

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Role · Context · Task · Format · Examples"]**

First, role: who the AI should act as. "Act as an experienced kindergarten teacher" gets you different advice from "act as a pediatric sleep consultant." Both are useful, for different problems.

Second, context: your situation. This is the most important part, and the one most people skip. Your assistant has never met you, so tell it who's involved, what you've already tried, and what constraints matter, like budget, time, skill level or tone.

Third, the task: exactly what you want. "Help me with my resume" is vague, but "Rewrite the summary section of my resume to highlight my leadership experience" is a task.

Fourth, format: the shape you want, like bullet points, a table, three options or under 150 words. This one part saves more editing time than any other.

And fifth, examples. If you have one of what good looks like, like a past email you liked, include it. Examples communicate style faster than any description.

### 3. Before and after · 1:35

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Write a message to my neighbor about their dog barking.
~~~

Let's see it in action. Here's the "before" prompt from the written lesson. It works, but you get a generic, slightly stiff note.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Act as a tactful communicator who helps neighbors stay on good terms.

Context: My next-door neighbor's dog barks most weekdays from about 8am
to noon while they're at work. I work from home, so it's hard to take
calls. We get along well and I don't want to sound like I'm complaining.

Task: Write a short, friendly text message that raises the issue and
suggests we figure out a solution together.

Format: Under 80 words. Warm and casual. No passive-aggressive phrasing.

Example of my usual tone: "Hey! Hope your weekend was good. Quick
question for you when you get a sec..."
~~~

Now the "after" version, in a new chat. You can see all five parts: a role, real context, a clear task, a format with a word limit and tone, and an example of the tone you want. This time you get a message you could send as is, that protects the relationship.

### 4. You don't need all five · 2:40

**[ON CAMERA]**

You don't need all five every time. A quick question might only need a task. But when an answer disappoints, the fix is almost always one of these five.

You don't need the labels either. Writing naturally works just as well, as long as the information is there. The written lesson has a reusable template with all five parts, so copy it into your notes app.

### 5. Pro tip: let the AI interview you · 3:05

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Not sure what to include? Let the AI interview you."]**

Here's a pro tip for when you don't know which context matters. Tell the AI your goal, and ask it to ask you up to five questions, all at once, before it answers. It turns the AI into an interviewer, so you end up with a great prompt without having to plan one.

### 6. Common mistakes · 3:30

The most common mistake is skipping context. The AI can't read your mind, and two extra sentences of background usually transform the answer. Also, don't ask for everything at once: "Plan my wedding" is too big, so break large jobs into steps.

And don't forget the format, or you'll get the AI's default, which is often longer than you want. Courtesy is fine, but clarity matters more.

### 7. Try it now · 4:00

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Try it now"]**

Your turn. Think of a real task you need to do this week, like a message, a plan or a decision. Write it as a one-line prompt, send it and save the answer.

Then rewrite it using all five parts and send it in a new chat. Compare the two answers side by side: which part made the biggest difference?

### 8. Recap and what's next · 4:25

**[ON CAMERA]**

Let's recap. Great prompts combine role, context, task, format and examples whenever the result matters. Context is the most powerful part, and the one most often skipped.

Specifying the format saves more editing time than anything else. And when you're unsure what to include, ask the AI to interview you first.
