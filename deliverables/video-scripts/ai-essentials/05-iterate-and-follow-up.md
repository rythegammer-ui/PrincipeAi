# Lesson 5: Iterate Like a Pro: Follow-Ups That Fix Any Answer

| | |
|---|---|
| **Course** | AI Essentials: Use AI in Everyday Life |
| **Module** | 2 · Prompting That Works |
| **Length** | 4–5 min (~600 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Turn a so-so first answer into exactly what you need with specific follow-ups, and know when to follow up, edit your prompt or start a fresh chat.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat.
- Have the birthday party prompt and its first follow-up from the written lesson's worked example ready to paste, in the same chat.
- Ask your editor to prepare the five follow-ups as a simple list graphic.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Iterate Like a Pro"]**

Here's something experienced AI users know: they rarely get a perfect answer on the first try either. What they do differently is what happens next. Instead of starting over or giving up, they steer.

A good follow-up takes ten seconds, and it often does more than a perfect first prompt.

### 2. Conversation, not vending machine · 0:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Think conversation, not vending machine"]**

A vending machine gives you one thing per button press. A conversation builds. Your assistant can see everything earlier in the chat, so you never need to repeat yourself.

Just react to what you got, the way you'd give feedback to a colleague. The simplest follow-ups are often the most effective, like "shorter," "more casual," "give me three different versions" or "explain that like I'm new to this."

### 3. Watch a plan take shape · 0:50

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Help me plan my daughter's 8th birthday party.
~~~

This is the worked example from the written lesson. The first answer is a generic checklist. It's fine, but it could be for anyone.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Budget is about $200, 12 kids, at our house, and she loves science.
~~~

One follow-up, and the plan becomes a science-themed party at home. Notice I didn't repeat anything, because the assistant still sees the whole conversation.

**[ON CAMERA]**

In the written lesson, two more follow-ups turn it into a timeline in 15-minute blocks, then a shopping list grouped by store section. Three short follow-ups, one genuinely useful plan, and each step built on the last.

### 4. Five follow-ups that do the heavy lifting · 1:55

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Say what's wrong and right · Ask for options · Ask for a self-critique · Add missing context · Ask what it needs"]**

The written lesson gives you five follow-ups that do the heavy lifting. First, say what's wrong, and what's right. "The ending sounds salesy" beats "make it better" every time.

Second, ask for options, because choosing is faster than describing what you want. Third, ask it to critique itself the way a tough editor would, then write an improved version. This works surprisingly well for writing, plans and arguments.

Fourth, add the context you forgot. And fifth, simply ask what information would help it make the answer much better.

### 5. Follow up, edit or start fresh? · 2:35

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Close? Follow up · Wrong direction? Edit or regenerate · Long and muddled? New chat"]**

So when should you follow up, and when should you start again? Follow up most of the time, when the answer is on the right track and just needs adjusting. If it went in the wrong direction from the start, edit your original message or regenerate, because fixing the prompt is cleaner than arguing with a bad answer.

And start a new chat when you're switching topics, or the conversation has become long and muddled. Here's a handy trick: before you leave, ask the old chat to summarize what you've worked out, including your preferences and decisions, so you can paste it into the new one.

### 6. Common mistakes · 3:20

**[ON CAMERA]**

The biggest mistake is starting over every time, which throws away all the context you built. Close behind is vague feedback: "That's not right" gives the AI nothing to work with, so say what's off and what you want instead.

And don't settle for the first draft. Two quick follow-ups usually lift an answer from okay to excellent.

### 7. Try it now · 3:45

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Try it now: three follow-ups"]**

Here's your exercise. Take an answer from an earlier lesson, or ask for something new, like a packing list or this week's to-do list. Improve it with at least three follow-ups: one about length or tone, one asking for options, and one asking the AI to critique itself.

Notice which follow-up helped most. That one goes in your toolkit.

### 8. Recap and what's next · 4:10

**[ON CAMERA]**

To recap: the first answer is a draft, and the real value is in the follow-up. Be specific about what to keep and what to change. Ask for options, ask for a self-critique, and ask what information is missing.

Follow up when you're close, and edit your prompt or start fresh when you're way off.
