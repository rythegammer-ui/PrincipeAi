# Lesson 6: Give AI Your Context: Custom Instructions, Projects & Templates

|---|---|

| **Length** | 4–5 min (~630 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Set up personal instructions, workspaces and saved templates so your assistant starts every conversation already knowing the basics about you.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat.
- Copy the personal instructions template from the written lesson and decide what you'll put in place of each bracketed part. Keep it general: no account numbers, health specifics or anything you wouldn't want stored.
- Optional: have a note called "My AI Prompts" open in your notes app to show briefly on screen.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Give AI Your Context"]**

If you've used AI for a week, you've probably typed the same background again and again: who you are, what you do, how you like your answers. There's a better way.

In this video, you'll see how to set things up once, so your assistant starts every conversation already knowing the basics.

### 2. Three layers of context · 0:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Personal instructions · Workspaces · Templates"]**

Think of context in three layers, from broadest to most specific. First, personal instructions: facts and preferences that apply to almost every chat. Second, workspaces: background for one ongoing area of your life, like a job search, a renovation or a course you're studying.

Third, templates: saved prompts for tasks you repeat. Assistants name these features differently, like custom instructions, personal preferences or projects, and features change over time. If yours doesn't have one of them, a note you paste in works everywhere.

### 3. Layer one: personal instructions · 1:00

Most assistants have a settings area where you can describe yourself and how you'd like it to respond. Whatever you write there is quietly included in your conversations.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
About me:
- I'm [ROLE OR LIFE SITUATION], based in [CITY OR COUNTRY].
- I mostly use AI for [YOUR TOP 3 USES].
- I'm [A BEGINNER / COMFORTABLE / AN EXPERT] with [RELEVANT TOPICS].

How I like answers:
- Get to the point: lead with the answer, then the detail.
- Use plain English and explain any jargon.
- Use bullet points for steps and lists.
- If my request is unclear, ask me a question before answering.
- If you're unsure about a fact, say so.
~~~

Here's the template from the written lesson, with my own details in place of the brackets. I'm pasting it at the start of a new chat, which works with any assistant, but if yours has a settings area for this, put it there instead.

Keep it short and general, and follow the postcard rule from Module 1: no sensitive details like account numbers or health specifics.

### 4. See the difference · 1:55

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Based on what you know about me, what are three ways you could save me time this week?
~~~

Now ask a question like this one, and check that the answer reflects what you told it.

That's the difference context makes. Ask a fresh assistant "How should I prepare for my meeting tomorrow?" and you'll get general tips. Ask one that knows you're a new team lead who prefers short bullet points and gets nervous presenting, and you'll get a focused prep checklist, with a few ways to calm your nerves.

### 5. Layer two: workspaces · 2:40

**[ON CAMERA]**

Layer two is workspaces. Some assistants let you group related chats into a project or workspace with its own instructions and files. That's perfect for anything that lasts weeks or months.

For a job search, you'd add your resume, a list of target roles and an instruction about the career change you're making, so every chat inside it, from a cover letter to interview practice, starts with the full picture. No workspace feature? Keep a short briefing note for each area, and paste it in when you start a related chat.

### 6. Layer three: templates · 3:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "My AI Prompts"]**

Layer three is templates. Any prompt you use more than twice deserves to be saved. Create a note called "My AI Prompts" in the notes app you already use, and store your best prompts with blanks to fill in, like a weekly plan or a reply helper.

### 7. Common mistakes · 3:40

A few mistakes to avoid. Writing an essay: long instructions get diluted, so keep only what applies to most chats. Setting and forgetting: review your instructions every couple of months as your needs change.

Storing sensitive details: save preferences, not private data. And never checking memory: if your assistant remembers things, look at what it saved now and then, and delete anything outdated.

### 8. Try it now · 4:05

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Try it now"]**

Your turn. Write your personal instructions from the template in the written lesson, five to ten lines, and add them to your assistant's settings or a note.

Then start a new chat, ask that "three ways to save me time" question, and check that the answer reflects your instructions. Finally, create your "My AI Prompts" note and save two templates.

### 9. Recap and what's next · 4:30

**[ON CAMERA]**

Let's recap. Layer your context: personal instructions, workspaces for ongoing areas, and saved templates. Keep personal instructions short, general and free of sensitive details.

And save any prompt you use more than twice, because your template library is your superpower.
