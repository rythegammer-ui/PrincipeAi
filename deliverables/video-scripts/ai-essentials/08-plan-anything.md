# Lesson 8: Plan Anything: Meals, Travel, Budgets & Your Week

**Goal of this video:** Use the goal + constraints + format formula to get meal, travel, budget and weekly plans that fit your real life, and know which details to confirm yourself.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat.
- Have the weekly planning prompt from the written lesson ready, and jot down what you'll put in place of each bracketed part: a week's tasks, appointments and deadlines, your fixed commitments, and when you have the most energy. A sample week is fine; leave out anything private.
- Have the "prepare tonight" question from the written lesson's Sunday reset ready to send in the same chat.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Plan Anything"]**

Planning is a hidden time sink. Deciding what's for dinner, mapping out a trip, working out where the money goes, squeezing a too-full week into seven days: none of it is hard, exactly, but it drains your energy.

AI is a remarkably good planning partner, as long as you give it the one thing it can't guess: your constraints.

### 2. The planning formula · 0:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Goal + Constraints + Format"]**

A plan is only useful if it fits your real life, so every planning prompt needs three things. First, your goal: what you want to end up with. Second, your constraints: budget, time, people, preferences, restrictions, and what you already have.

Third, the format, meaning how you'll use the plan, like a shopping list, a day-by-day itinerary or a table. The more constraints you share, the less you'll have to fix later.

### 3. Plan your week · 0:55

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Here's everything on my plate this week: [TASKS, APPOINTMENTS AND
DEADLINES]. Fixed commitments: [MEETINGS, SCHOOL RUNS, ETC.]. I have
the most energy in the [MORNING / AFTERNOON].

Build a realistic day-by-day plan. Put demanding work in my high-energy
time, batch small tasks, leave buffer time, and tell me honestly what
won't fit, with suggestions for what to drop, delay or delegate.
~~~

Here's the weekly planning prompt from the written lesson, with a week's worth of tasks and commitments in place of the brackets. Look at that last line. A plan that pretends everything fits isn't a plan, and asking AI to be honest about capacity gives you permission to let something go.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
What's one thing I could prepare tonight to make this week easier?
~~~

This question is part of the Sunday reset from the written lesson, a 15-minute weekly routine. You brain-dump your week, ask for dinners that fit the busy evenings and one grocery list for the week, then finish with this. Four prompts, one calmer week.

### 4. Meals, travel and budgets · 2:05

**[ON CAMERA]**

The written lesson has a ready-made prompt for each area. For meals, include how many people, dietary needs and dislikes, cooking time and what you already have, and ask for one combined grocery list. Then follow up with something like "Swap Wednesday for something vegetarian" or "Which of these can I prep on Sunday?"

A note on allergies: AI can suggest recipes that avoid an allergen, but always read the labels yourself. It can't see what's actually in the product you buy.

For travel, AI is great at turning a vague idea into a day-by-day itinerary. But verify before you book: opening hours, prices, seasonal closures, transport timetables and entry or visa requirements change, so confirm details on official websites. If your assistant can search the web, ask it to include links, then open them.

For budgets, the prompt asks AI to sort your expenses into needs, wants and savings, suggest realistic places to cut, and show its math. Use rounded figures and categories, never account numbers, logins or full statements. And double-check the totals with a calculator, because it takes ten seconds to be sure.

### 5. Common mistakes · 3:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "No constraints = generic plan"]**

The most common mistake is no constraints. "Plan a trip to Italy" gets a generic tourist list, while who, when, budget and pace change everything.

Also watch out for trusting time-sensitive details, and confirm anything you'll pay for or travel for. Ask for buffer time and honest trade-offs, so your plans aren't over-packed. And don't share sensitive financial details, because categories and rounded numbers are all AI needs.

### 6. Try it now · 3:55

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Try it now: at least four constraints"]**

Your turn. Pick one area: meals, a trip, your budget or your week. Write down at least four constraints before you write the prompt.

Then use the matching prompt in the written lesson, and improve the plan with two follow-ups. Save your final prompt to your "My AI Prompts" note so you can reuse it.

### 7. Recap and what's next · 4:20

**[ON CAMERA]**

To recap: every good planning prompt includes a goal, your constraints and a useful format. AI drafts the plan, and you confirm prices, hours, rules and anything you'll pay for.

Ask AI to tell you honestly what won't fit. And a 15-minute weekly planning session with AI can make the whole week calmer.
