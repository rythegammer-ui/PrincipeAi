# Lesson 9: Learn Anything Faster with an AI Tutor

| | |
|---|---|
| **Course** | AI Essentials: Use AI in Everyday Life |
| **Module** | 3 · AI for Everyday Life |
| **Length** | 4–5 min (~610 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Use AI in tutor mode to understand new topics at your level, test yourself and make what you learn stick.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat.
- Pick one well-established topic to learn about on screen, such as one of the examples from the written lesson: how interest rates work or how to prune roses.
- Have the "explain it at my level" and "quiz me" prompts from the written lesson ready, with your topic in place of the placeholder. Send both in the same chat, and answer the first quiz question on screen.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Learn Anything Faster with an AI Tutor"]**

Imagine a tutor who knows something about almost every subject, never gets impatient, is available at 2am, and will happily explain the same idea five different ways until it clicks. That's what an AI assistant can be for you.

But only if you use it to learn, not just to get answers.

### 2. Shortcut mode vs. tutor mode · 0:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Shortcut mode: What's the answer? · Tutor mode: Help me understand this."]**

There are two ways to use AI for learning. Shortcut mode is "What's the answer?" It's fast, but very little sticks.

Tutor mode is "Help me understand this." It's a little slower, and you actually learn. That's because learning takes effort, like explaining, recalling, making mistakes and correcting them, and a good AI tutor makes you do that work, with support.

### 3. Explain it at my level · 0:50

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Explain [TOPIC] to me. I'm a complete beginner. Start with the big
picture in 3-4 sentences, use an everyday analogy, then give me the
3 most important ideas I need to understand first.
~~~

Technique one: explain it at my level. This prompt tells the AI I'm a complete beginner, then asks for the big picture, an everyday analogy and the three most important ideas to understand first. Always say your level, or the explanation may be pitched too high or too low.

Not clicking yet? Follow up with "Explain it a different way," "Give me a real-world example" or "What's a common misconception about this?"

### 4. Quiz me · 1:35

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Quiz me on [TOPIC] with 5 questions, one at a time. Wait for my answer
before moving on. If I get one wrong, give me a hint before the answer.
At the end, tell me which areas I should review.
~~~

Technique two: quiz me. Testing yourself is one of the most effective ways to remember things, and learning researchers call it retrieval practice. Notice it asks one question at a time and waits for my answer, with a hint before the answer if I get one wrong.

### 5. Three more techniques · 2:10

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Teach it back · Socratic tutor · Learning plan"]**

Technique three: teach it back. If you can explain something simply, you understand it, so explain the topic in your own words and ask the AI what you got right, what you got wrong or left out.

Technique four is the Socratic tutor. Instead of giving you answers, it asks guiding questions, one at a time, that help you work it out yourself. It's slower, and it's also how the deepest learning happens.

And technique five: ask for a week-by-week learning plan, based on your goal, your timeframe and the time you have each week. All five prompts are in the written lesson.

Learning a language? The written lesson also has a prompt that turns AI into a patient conversation partner, one that gently corrects your mistakes after each reply.

### 6. Students and accuracy · 3:05

A note for students and parents. Using AI to understand material, practice and get feedback is powerful, but using it to write your assignments usually breaks the rules and skips the learning. Check your school's policy, and use AI the way you'd use a great tutor, not a ghostwriter.

And watch for accuracy. AI is very reliable on well-established topics, but less reliable on niche, fast-changing or highly technical specifics. If details matter, as with a certification exam, a medical topic or a legal requirement, confirm key facts with your textbook, official guidance or another trusted source.

### 7. Try it now · 3:45

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Try it now: explain · quiz · teach it back"]**

Your turn. Pick something you've always wanted to understand better, anything from how interest rates work to how to prune roses. Use the "explain it at my level" prompt, then the quiz prompt, and answer all five questions honestly.

Finally, explain the topic back in your own words and ask for feedback. Don't just read passively, because stopping to explain things back is where the learning happens.

### 8. Recap and what's next · 4:15

**[ON CAMERA]**

Let's recap. Use AI in tutor mode, focused on understanding, rather than shortcut mode. Ask for explanations at your level, then quiz yourself and teach it back.

A Socratic tutor makes you think, and thinking is what makes learning stick. And verify key facts when you're learning niche or high-stakes topics.
