**Goal of this video:** Use AI to understand and prepare for health, legal and money questions with the traffic-light system, and know when to verify or see a professional.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat.
- Prepare a short, generic clause from a sample lease for the demo, with no names, addresses or account numbers in it. Fill in the prompt's brackets with "lease", that clause and your state or country.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Health, Legal & Money Questions: Using AI Safely"]**

Sooner or later you'll want to ask AI about something serious. A symptom, a confusing clause in a lease, or whether to pay off debt or save. AI can be genuinely helpful here, but it can also be confidently wrong in ways that matter.

This lesson gives you a simple system for getting the benefits safely.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Emergency? Contact emergency services, not AI"]**

But first, the most important rule. If you or someone else might be in danger, or there are symptoms like chest pain, difficulty breathing or signs of a stroke, contact emergency services right away. Don't ask an AI.

### 2. Why these topics are different · 0:40

With everyday tasks, a mistake costs you a rewrite. Here, it can cost your health, your money or your legal rights. Three reasons to be extra careful:

First, AI can't see your full situation. A doctor examines you, a lawyer reads your documents, and a financial advisor knows your whole picture, but AI only knows what you typed.

Second, rules depend on where you live. Laws, taxes, benefits and medical guidelines vary by country, state and even city, and AI may blend them together.

Third, it tends to reassure. Assistants are built to be helpful and agreeable, so they can lean toward telling you what you hope to hear.

### 3. The traffic-light system · 1:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Green: go · Yellow: verify · Red: see a professional"]**

Here's the system: think of a traffic light.

Green is explaining terms, summarizing documents and preparing questions. Go ahead. Yellow is general guidance, typical options and how a process usually works, so verify it with an official source.

Red is diagnosis, medication changes, legal strategy and big money decisions. Prepare with AI, and decide with a professional. The safest and most valuable use is green: understanding and preparing.

### 4. Demo: understand before you sign · 1:55

**[CUT TO SCREEN]**

Let me show you a green-light question, using a short clause from a sample lease with nothing personal in it.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Here's a clause from my [LEASE / CONTRACT / TERMS], with names and
addresses removed: [PASTE CLAUSE]. I'm in [STATE OR COUNTRY].
1. Explain what it means in plain English.
2. What could it mean for me in practice? Give one example.
3. What questions should I ask the other party, or a lawyer, about it?
~~~

Notice the prompt removes names and addresses, and says where you are, because rules vary so much.

Back comes a plain-English explanation, a practical example and questions for the other party or a lawyer. It helps you understand without pretending to decide for you. But AI can't confirm that a contract is fair or enforceable where you live.

The written lesson has similar templates for a doctor's appointment and a money decision. They include lines like "Don't diagnose me" and "Don't tell me what to do," so you get understanding, not a verdict.

**[ON CAMERA]**

For anything with real stakes, like a dispute, a large contract or an immigration matter, talk to a qualified lawyer. Many areas have free or low-cost legal aid services. And with money, check any numbers with a calculator, and confirm current rates, fees and tax rules with official sources.

### 5. The biggest mistake · 3:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Ask for understanding, not a verdict"]**

The most common mistake is asking for a verdict instead of understanding. "Should I sign this?" is red. "What does this clause mean, and what should I ask?" is green.

Two more habits: always say where you are, and anonymize first. Strip out names, dates of birth, addresses, account numbers, policy numbers and medical record numbers before you paste anything.

"A 45-year-old with type 2 diabetes" is all the AI needs, not your patient ID. Finally, don't treat reassurance as the all-clear. If something feels wrong with your health, money or legal situation, a professional opinion beats a reassuring paragraph.

### 6. Try it now · 4:05

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Try it now: decode a confusing document"]**

Find a document with confusing language, like an insurance policy, a bill, a lease or a benefits letter. Remove the personal details, paste a short section, and ask for a plain-English explanation and three questions.

Then decide which traffic light each of your follow-up questions falls under, and where you'd verify the yellow ones. The steps are in the written lesson.

### 7. Recap and what's next · 4:30

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Understand · Organize · Prepare"]**

So, to recap. Use AI to understand, organize and prepare. Follow the traffic lights: green means go, yellow means verify, and red means see a professional.

Always include your location, and always remove identifying details. And in an emergency, contact emergency services, never an AI.

In the next lesson, Write and Edit Better (Without Sounding Like a Robot), you'll learn to use AI as a sharp editor, so your writing gets clearer while still sounding like you. See you there.
