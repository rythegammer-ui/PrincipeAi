# Lesson 13: Better Decisions and Brainstorming

| | |
|---|---|
| **Course** | AI Essentials: Use AI in Everyday Life |
| **Module** | 4 · AI for Work & Productivity |
| **Length** | 4–5 min (~590 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Use AI as a thinking partner to brainstorm more ideas, untangle a decision with a weighted comparison, and spot blind spots, while keeping the final call yours.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat.
- Pick a sample goal for the brainstorm and fill the brackets with who it's for, your constraints, a budget and anything already ruled out.
- Pick a sample decision for the weighted comparison: two options and three or four criteria. The job-offer example from the written lesson (income, time with family, growth and stress) works well.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Better Decisions and Brainstorming"]**

Some of the best uses of AI don't produce anything you'd send or publish. They produce better thinking.

Stuck for ideas? AI can give you twenty in a minute.

Torn between two options? It can help you see trade-offs you've been too close to notice.

In this lesson you'll learn to use AI as a thinking partner: creative, honest, and never the one making the decision.

### 2. Brainstorm: go wide, then narrow · 0:30

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Diverge, then converge"]**

Good brainstorming has two phases. First you diverge, with lots of ideas and no judging. Then you converge and pick the best.

AI is brilliant at the first and helpful with the second. Here's the prompt for going wide.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
I need ideas for [GOAL]. Context: [WHO IT'S FOR, CONSTRAINTS, BUDGET,
AND WHAT I'VE ALREADY TRIED OR RULED OUT].
Give me 20 ideas: some safe, some unusual, and a few that seem almost
too bold. One line each.
~~~

Notice that it asks for quantity. The first few ideas are usually the obvious ones, and the interesting ones come later in the list.

Then narrow it down. Ask for the five that best fit your constraints, each with its biggest advantage, its biggest risk and a cheap way to test it. You can also push the edges with "What would this look like with half the budget?"

### 3. Decide: the weighted comparison · 1:30

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Structure beats agonizing"]**

When you're stuck on a decision, it's often because the options are tangled up in your head. AI helps you untangle them.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Help me decide between [OPTION A] and [OPTION B].
What matters most to me, in order: [CRITERIA, E.G. COST, COMMUTE,
GROWTH, FAMILY TIME].
Create a table scoring each option 1-5 on each criterion, with a
one-line reason for each score. Then tell me which option comes out
ahead, and what would have to be true for the other one to be better.
~~~

You get a table with a score and a reason for each criterion. But the powerful part is the last question, because it shows you which assumptions the decision really hinges on.

**[ON CAMERA]**

Picture a job offer with higher pay and a longer commute. The new job wins on income and growth, and your current job wins on family time. Then that last question reveals the decision hinges on whether you could work from home two days a week.

That's not a question for AI. It's a question for the hiring manager, and now you know exactly what to ask.

**[OPTIONAL PERSONAL EXAMPLE: a decision you were stuck on, where laying out the trade-offs showed you what it really depended on.]**

### 4. Find your blind spots · 2:35

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Pre-mortem · Honest challenger"]**

Two more tools are in the written lesson. The pre-mortem asks AI to imagine it's a year from now and your decision turned out to be a mistake. It gives you the likely reasons, and one thing you could do now to prevent each.

And because assistants tend to agree with you, use the honest challenger. Ask for the strongest honest case against what you're leaning toward, without softening it.

### 5. Mistakes to avoid · 3:05

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "AI structures. You decide."]**

The most common mistake is asking, "What should I do?" You'll get a generic answer, so ask for structure, trade-offs and blind spots instead.

Another is leading the witness. "Isn't option A better?" invites agreement, so ask neutrally. And don't outsource the decision, because AI only knows what you value if you tell it.

Know when to stop, too. If you've done a comparison and a pre-mortem and still can't decide, the options may be genuinely close. Gather one more piece of real-world information, sleep on it, or talk it through with someone who knows you.

### 6. Try it now · 3:45

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Try it now: compare, then pre-mortem"]**

Pick a real decision you're weighing, big or small. Run the weighted comparison with your real criteria, then run the pre-mortem on your leading option.

Finally, write down the one question or fact the decision hinges on, and go and find the answer. Both prompts are in the written lesson.

### 7. Recap and what's next · 4:10

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Go wide · Weigh it · Test it · You decide"]**

To recap. Brainstorm in two phases: go wide, then narrow with clear criteria. A weighted comparison untangles your options and shows what the decision hinges on.

Run a pre-mortem, and ask for the strongest case against your favorite option. AI structures the thinking, and you make the call.
