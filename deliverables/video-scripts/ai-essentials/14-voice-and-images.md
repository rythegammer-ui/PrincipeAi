# Lesson 14: Talk, Snap & Show: Voice and Image Features

| | |
|---|---|
| **Course** | AI Essentials: Use AI in Everyday Life |
| **Module** | 4 · AI for Work & Productivity |
| **Length** | 4–5 min (~580 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Go beyond typing: use voice for brain dumps and rehearsals, ask clear questions about photos, and describe images you want created, while checking details and protecting privacy.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat, and check which voice and image features your assistant and plan offer.
- For the first demo, prepare a photo of a sample letter that's fine to show on screen, with names, addresses and account numbers covered or cropped out. Attach it in place of the first bracket and name the letter's topic in the second.
- Record the image-creation demo in an assistant that can create images. If yours can't, record that part in one that can.
- Optional B-roll: you talking to your phone while cooking or walking.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Talk, Snap & Show: Voice and Image Features"]**

Typing isn't the only way to use AI anymore. Many assistants can listen and talk back, look at a photo you've taken, and even create images from a description.

That turns AI into something you can use while cooking, walking, or standing in front of a confusing parking sign.

Features vary between assistants and plans, and they change quickly, so check what yours offers. The techniques in this lesson work wherever the features are available.

### 2. Voice: think out loud · 0:30

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Dictation · Voice conversation"]**

There are usually two kinds of voice feature. Dictation types your words into the message box, which is great for long, rambling context. Voice conversation is a spoken back-and-forth, a bit like a phone call with your assistant.

**[B-ROLL: talking to a phone while cooking]**

Voice shines when your hands are busy or your thoughts are messy. Try a brain dump: talk for two minutes about everything on your mind, then say, "Turn that into an organized to-do list, grouped by priority."

You can also rehearse out loud. The written lesson has a spoken prompt where the AI plays your manager, pushes back a little, then tells you what you did well and what to improve.

### 3. Demo: AI that can see · 1:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Photo + a clear question"]**

Many assistants can also look at a photo, like your fridge, a confusing form, a screenshot of an error message or handwritten notes. A good image prompt still uses the five parts, so add context and a clear task.

Here I'm attaching a photo of a sample letter, with the personal details covered.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
[ATTACH PHOTO] This is a letter I received about [TOPIC]. In plain
English: what is it about, do I need to do anything, and by when?
Flag anything that looks unusual or like a possible scam.
~~~

Look at how the answer follows the task: what the letter is about, whether I need to act, by when, and anything that looks unusual.

### 4. Check what it read · 2:05

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Check important details against the original"]**

Now the most common mistake: assuming it read everything correctly. AI can misread blurry text, small numbers and handwriting. For anything important, like a medication label, a bill amount or a deadline, check against the original.

Identifying plants, mushrooms, insects or skin conditions from a photo is a starting point only. And never eat a wild plant or mushroom based on an AI identification.

### 5. Demo: creating images · 2:35

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Subject · Style · Composition and mood · Use"]**

Some assistants can also create images, like a party invitation or an illustration for a presentation. Describe four things: the subject, the style, the composition and mood, and how you'll use it.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Create an image for a kids' science birthday party invitation: a
friendly cartoon rocket and planets, bright colors, playful style,
with empty space at the top for text. Square format.
~~~

All four parts are there. And notice the empty space at the top, because image generators can still struggle with exact wording. Add any text yourself in a design or document app.

### 6. Privacy and respect · 3:15

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Crop first. Ask first."]**

Photos can show more than you meant to share, like faces, addresses, license plates and paperwork in the background. So crop first.

Don't create images of real people in ways that could mislead or embarrass them. And ask before uploading other people's photos, because your friend's face isn't yours to share.

### 7. Try it now · 3:40

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Try it now: snap, ask, talk"]**

Take a photo of something real, like your fridge, a sign, or a confusing piece of mail with personal details covered. Ask a specific question about it, with context and a clear task.

If your assistant has voice, try a two-minute spoken brain dump and ask it to organize your thoughts. The steps are in the written lesson.

### 8. Recap and what's next · 4:05

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Talk it through · Snap and ask · Check the details"]**

To recap. Voice is perfect for brain dumps, rehearsals and hands-free help. A photo plus a clear question makes forms, letters, labels and screenshots easy to understand.

Check anything important against the original, and never rely on AI to tell you what's safe to eat. Crop out sensitive details, and never create misleading images of real people.
