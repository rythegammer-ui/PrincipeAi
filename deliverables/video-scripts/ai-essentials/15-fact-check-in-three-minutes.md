# Lesson 15: Fact-Check AI in Three Minutes

| | |
|---|---|
| **Course** | AI Essentials: Use AI in Everyday Life |
| **Module** | 5 · Make It a Habit |
| **Length** | 4–5 min (~590 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Run a fast, four-step fact-check that matches your effort to the stakes and catches AI mistakes before they cost you.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat and ask it a factual question first, such as how a rule works where you live or a health guideline, so there's an answer on screen for the demo.
- Have one official or original source open in another tab, so you can show checking a claim against it.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Fact-Check AI in Three Minutes"]**

You know AI can be confidently wrong. But you can't verify every sentence, or AI would stop saving you time.

The answer is a fast, focused routine: check the right things, in the right way, in about three minutes. Use it whenever an answer will inform a decision, go out under your name, or cost money.

### 2. Step 1: does it need checking? · 0:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "What happens if this is wrong?"]**

Step one takes ten seconds. Ask yourself one question: "What happens if this is wrong?"

For low stakes, like a brainstorm or a draft you'll edit, a quick read-through is enough. For medium stakes, like an email with facts in it or a plan you'll follow, check the key claims.

And for high stakes, like health, legal, money, safety or anything published, check every specific claim and consider a professional.

### 3. Step 2: spot the checkable claims · 0:55

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Errors hide in specifics"]**

Step two takes about thirty seconds. Errors hide in specifics: names, numbers, dates, quotes, rules, links, citations, and anything that surprised you.

You can scan for those yourself, or let the AI do the scanning. I've already asked a factual question, so here's the follow-up.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
List every specific factual claim in your last answer: names, numbers,
dates, quotes, sources and rules. For each one, tell me how confident
you are (high, medium or low) and the best type of source to verify it.
~~~

Now there's a list of claims, each with a confidence level and the best type of source to check it. That tells you where to spend your next two minutes.

### 4. Step 3: verify the important ones · 1:40

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Go to the source"]**

Step three is two minutes of real checking. Go to the source, because official websites beat blogs and forums, and the original study beats news coverage of it.

Open every link and make sure it says what the AI claimed. Search for the exact quote or title, and if you can't find it anywhere, treat it as invented. Redo important math, and check the date, because prices and rules change fast.

A handy technique here is lateral reading. Instead of studying one source closely, open a few others in new tabs and see what they say about the same claim. If reliable sources agree, you're in good shape.

### 5. Step 4: let AI help check itself · 2:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Self-checks find problems. Sources settle them."]**

Step four: let AI help check itself, carefully. These prompts help surface problems, but they don't replace real sources.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Argue against your own answer. What would a skeptical expert say?
~~~

You can also get a second opinion. Paste the claim into a different assistant or a fresh chat and ask, "Is this accurate? What's the evidence?"

If the answers disagree, you know exactly where to dig.

### 6. The mistake and the red flags · 3:05

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Check a real source, not just the AI"]**

The most common mistake is asking, "Are you sure?" and stopping there. The AI might double down on a mistake, or back down when it was right. Check a real source.

And slow down when you see red flags. Watch for very precise statistics with no source, quotes that are a little too perfect, and citations you can't find.

Be just as careful with information that only confirms what you hoped, and with confident answers about very recent events.

### 7. Try it now · 3:40

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Try it now: check two claims"]**

Ask your assistant a factual question you're genuinely curious about, like a historical event, a health guideline or how a rule works where you live. Then use the "list every specific factual claim" prompt.

Verify the two most important claims using official or original sources. Then note what you found: all correct, partly outdated, or wrong? The steps are in the written lesson.

### 8. Recap and what's next · 4:05

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Stakes · Specifics · Sources"]**

To recap. Match your checking effort to the stakes by asking what happens if this is wrong. Errors hide in specifics: names, numbers, dates, quotes, links and rules.

Verify with official and original sources, open every link, and redo important math. AI self-checks and second opinions help you find problems, but real sources settle them.

You can't verify every sentence, but you can check the right things in about three minutes.
