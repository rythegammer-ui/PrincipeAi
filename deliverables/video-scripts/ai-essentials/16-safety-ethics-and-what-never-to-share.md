# Lesson 16: Safety, Ethics & What Never to Share

**Goal of this video:** Know what never to paste into an AI assistant, recognize AI-powered scams and verify them safely, and use AI honestly and accountably.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat.
- Write a short, made-up sample scam text to paste in place of the bracket, with typical red flags: urgency, secrecy, a request to pay by gift card and a "verify your account" link. Use no real names, numbers, companies or working links.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Safety, Ethics & What Never to Share"]**

AI is a powerful tool, and like any powerful tool, it comes with a few rules worth knowing by heart.

This lesson covers three things: protecting your information, protecting yourself from people who use AI against you, and using AI in a way you'd be comfortable explaining to anyone.

### 2. What never to share · 0:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Never paste passwords, financial or ID numbers, health records or confidential data"]**

You learned the postcard rule in Module 1. Here's the complete list: never paste passwords, PINs, security codes or recovery codes. Never paste full card numbers, bank details or government ID numbers.

The same goes for medical record numbers, insurance policy numbers and detailed health records tied to your name. Don't paste other people's private information either. And keep confidential work material out, unless your employer has approved the tool for it.

Before you paste anything, pause and ask three questions.

Would I be comfortable if a stranger read this? Does it identify me or someone else? Could I describe it instead of pasting it?

You can almost always get the same help by swapping specifics for descriptions: "my client" instead of a name, "a mid-sized city" instead of an address, and rounded numbers instead of exact balances.

### 3. Scams just got smarter · 1:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Agree on a family code word"]**

Scammers use AI too, and their messages are now well written, personalized and convincing. Here are three threats to know.

First, voice cloning. Scammers can imitate someone's voice from a short recording, so a panicked call from a "grandchild" or "boss" asking for urgent money could be fake. Agree on a family code word, and if a call feels urgent and unusual, hang up and call back on a number you already know.

Second, deepfake images and video. Be skeptical of shocking content, especially when it pushes you to act or share right away.

Third, polished phishing. Bad spelling used to be a giveaway, but not anymore. Judge a message by what it asks for: urgency, secrecy, payment by gift card, wire transfer or crypto, or a link to "verify your account."

### 4. Demo: check a suspicious message · 2:15

AI can help you spot scams, too. I'll use a made-up sample message with no personal details in it.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
I received this message and I'm not sure it's legitimate. I haven't
clicked anything. [PASTE THE TEXT, WITHOUT PERSONAL DETAILS]
What are the warning signs, if any? How can I verify it safely without
using any of the contact details in the message?
~~~

The answer walks through the warning signs and safe ways to check. Then verify through official contact details you look up yourself, never the ones in the message.

### 5. Using AI ethically · 2:50

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "If you send it, you own it"]**

Now, using AI ethically. Be honest about AI help when it matters, and follow any rules your school, employer, clients or publisher have. And stay accountable, because if you send it, you own it.

Watch for bias, especially when AI output affects people, like hiring, reviews or descriptions of others. Respect creators, and never use AI to impersonate a real person or to create fake reviews, fake documents or misleading images.

Keep your own skills sharp, too, and remember that AI isn't a therapist or a friend. It can be a helpful sounding board on a hard day, but it's no substitute for real people or professional support. If you're struggling, reach out to someone you trust or a mental health professional, and in a crisis, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line.

### 6. Try it now · 3:50

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Try it now: settings · code word · your three rules"]**

Three quick tasks. Revisit your assistant's training, memory and history settings, and agree on a code word with your family or closest people. Then write your own three-line AI rules: what you'll never share, when you'll mention AI help, and what you'll always verify.

One common mistake to avoid while you're in those settings: assuming deleted means gone everywhere. Deleting a chat removes it from your history, but retention policies vary, so don't share what you'd regret.

### 7. Recap and what's next · 4:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Describe, don't paste · Verify through trusted channels · Own what you send"]**

To recap. Never share passwords, financial or ID numbers, health records or confidential data, and describe instead of pasting. Scams now use cloned voices, deepfakes and polished messages, so verify through channels you already trust.

Be honest about AI help when it matters, and stay accountable for anything you send. Use AI to support your thinking and your relationships, not to replace them.
