# Lesson 17: Build Your Personal AI Toolkit

| | |
|---|---|
| **Course** | AI Essentials: Use AI in Everyday Life |
| **Module** | 5 · Make It a Habit |
| **Length** | 4–5 min (~590 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Pull the course together into a simple personal toolkit: your assistant, your instructions, a saved prompt library, your workspaces and your safety routine.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat.
- For the first demo, prepare a short sample "typical week" to fill the bracket: a few work, home and recurring tasks, plus something you dread.
- For the second demo, pick any prompt from this course (the edit-for-clarity prompt works well) and a sample of what you'd usually have to fix in its answers.
- Optional B-roll: a notes app showing a note titled "My AI Prompts".

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Build Your Personal AI Toolkit"]**

You've learned a lot of techniques. The risk now is the same as with any course: you finish, feel inspired for a week, then slowly drift back to old habits.

The fix is to turn what you've learned into a small personal toolkit. That's a few things set up and saved, so using AI well becomes the easy default.

### 2. The five parts of your toolkit · 0:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Assistant · Instructions · Prompt library · Workspaces · Safety routine"]**

Your toolkit has five parts. First, your assistant: one main assistant you know well, plus a second for second opinions if you like. Second, your personal instructions, the short "about me and how I like answers" text from Module 2.

Third, your prompt library: eight to twelve saved prompts for the things you do most. Fourth, your workspaces, one for each ongoing area of life or work, if your assistant supports them. And fifth, your safety routine: what you never share, and your three-minute fact-check.

### 3. Demo: find your best uses · 1:00

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Language-heavy? Would a draft help? Can I check it?"]**

Not everything needs AI. The best candidates pass three tests: is it language-heavy, would a good first draft help, and can I check the result? Let AI help you find them.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Help me find my best uses for AI. Here's a typical week for me:
[WORK, HOME, ROUTINES, RECURRING TASKS AND THINGS I DREAD].
Identify the 5 tasks where AI could save me the most time or stress.
For each, explain how, and give me a ready-to-use prompt template with
[BRACKETS] for the details that change each time.
~~~

With a sample week filled in, you get five tasks, each with an explanation and a template ready to save. Pick your top three.

### 4. Build your prompt library · 1:40

**[B-ROLL: a notes app with a "My AI Prompts" note]**

Now save them in a "My AI Prompts" note. A good entry has a clear name, like "Weekly plan" or "Reply helper," so you can find it fast. Then comes the prompt itself, with brackets for anything that changes, and a note on any follow-up that usually helps.

The written lesson has a starter library drawn from this whole course, from appointment prep to the pre-mortem. Pick the ones that fit your life.

And keep it short. The most common mistake is collecting prompts you never use, and a short library you use beats a huge one you don't.

### 5. Demo: make your templates better · 2:25

When you notice you're fixing the same thing every time, improve the prompt. AI can help with that, too.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Here's a prompt I use regularly: [PASTE PROMPT]. Here's what I usually
have to fix in the answers: [ISSUES]. Improve the prompt so I get better
results the first time. Keep it short and keep the [BRACKETS].
~~~

The goal is a prompt that gets it right the first time, with the brackets still in place so you can reuse it.

### 6. Remove the friction · 2:55

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Context in. Check specifics. Never paste passwords."]**

Small setup choices decide whether you actually use AI day to day. Pin your assistant to your phone's home screen and your browser's bookmarks bar, and keep your prompt library one tap away in the notes app you already open every day. Use dictation when typing out context feels like a chore.

Picture a freelance designer with two kids. Their toolkit is one main assistant, short personal instructions, nine saved prompts, one workspace for the biggest client project, and a sticky note on the monitor.

The note says, "Context in. Check specifics. Never paste passwords."

That's simple enough to use daily, and powerful enough to save real time every week. And don't chase every new tool, because mastering one assistant beats dabbling in five.

### 7. Try it now · 3:50

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Try it now: build your toolkit"]**

Run the "find my best uses" prompt and pick your top three. Then create or update your "My AI Prompts" note with at least eight prompts.

Review and refresh your personal instructions. Finally, write a one-line rule for safe use, and put it somewhere you'll see it.

### 8. Recap and what's next · 4:10

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Five parts · Saved prompts · Less friction"]**

To recap. Your toolkit has five parts: assistant, personal instructions, prompt library, workspaces and safety routine. Focus AI on tasks that are language-heavy, benefit from a draft, and that you can check.

Save prompts with brackets and keep improving them. And remove friction, so using AI well becomes your default.

In the next lesson, Your 30-Day AI Habit Plan, you'll turn this toolkit into a lasting habit, in about ten minutes a day. See you there.
