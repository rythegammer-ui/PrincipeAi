# Lesson 4: Choose Your First Three Projects

| | |
|---|---|
| **Course** | AI for Business Owners: The Implementation Playbook |
| **Module** | 1 · The AI Opportunity Audit |
| **Length** | 5–6 min (~720 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Viewers can choose a balanced first three AI projects (a quick win, a core workflow and a growth bet), test them against the selection filters, and draft a one-page brief for each.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat.
- Pick one project to use in the demo, from your own shortlist or a clearly hypothetical business, and jot down a few lines on how it's done today, who does it, how often and how long it takes.
- Copy the "Use AI to draft your briefs" prompt from the written lesson, ready to paste.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

The top of your scored list is a shortlist, not a plan. Choosing well matters more than it looks, because your first projects shape how your whole team feels about AI.

A visible early win builds momentum. A messy, overambitious first project can set adoption back by months.

### 2. Why three, and not ten · 0:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Three projects, not ten"]**

So why three? Each project needs an owner, a little setup, testing and a change in someone's habits.

Three in parallel is about as much as a small business can carry while still running day to day. Start ten, and you'll likely finish none.

### 3. The balanced portfolio · 0:40

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Quick win · Core workflow · Growth bet"]**

Pick one project from each of three categories. First, the quick win, live within days. It's high ease and low risk, like meeting summaries or first drafts of inquiry replies, and it proves AI works in your business.

Second, the core workflow, over two to four weeks. That's a high-hours process that eats real time every week, like quotes and proposals, weekly client updates or writing SOPs.

Third, the growth bet, also two to four weeks. It's tied to revenue, like consistent lead follow-up, reactivating past customers or a content engine that keeps you visible.

That mix gives you an early success, meaningful time back and a shot at measurable growth, without betting everything on one idea.

### 4. A hypothetical portfolio · 1:30

Picture a hypothetical four-person bookkeeping firm. Its quick win is client emails explaining common topics, drafted from a library of approved explanations, with a partner reviewing every one. Its core workflow is monthly report commentary, where AI drafts a plain-English summary and the accountant checks every figure against the report.

Its growth bet is a quarterly newsletter and a friendly check-in email to past clients. And notice what's missing: anything where AI gives financial advice or touches raw client data in an unapproved tool.

### 5. The selection filters · 2:05

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Owner · Baseline · Digital inputs · Quick review · Cheap failure · Data rules"]**

Before you commit, run every project through the filters in the written lesson. There's a named owner who does this work today and wants it improved. You can measure a baseline now, and the inputs are digital or easy to make digital.

A person can review the output in much less time than creating it, and failure would be cheap and reversible. And it needs no sensitive data, or you have an approved tool and process for that data.

If a project fails a check, either fix the gap or swap in the next project on your list.

### 6. Demo: draft your project brief · 2:50

Every project then gets a one-page brief. It turns a good idea into a project someone can actually run, and you can draft it with AI.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Help me write a one-page project brief for an AI pilot in my business.

Project: [NAME]
What happens today, step by step: [DESCRIBE THE CURRENT PROCESS]
Who does it, how often and how long it takes: [DETAILS]

Before writing, ask me up to eight questions to fill any gaps. Then produce the brief with these sections: Payoff type, Problem today, Current process, New process with AI, Owner, Tools, Baseline metric, Target, Human review rule, Data rules, What done looks like, Review date.

Keep the new process realistic for off-the-shelf AI tools and a small team, and flag anything that sounds risky.
~~~

I'll fill in the project name, the current process, and who does it, how often and how long it takes. Notice that the AI asks up to eight questions before it writes anything, so answer them properly.

When the brief comes back, check the new process. It should show exactly where AI drafts and where a person reviews, and it should name one owner, not a committee. Then look closely at anything it flags as risky.

### 7. Avoid the classic traps · 3:45

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Earn the right to automate"]**

Now, the classic first-project traps. The first is a fully automated, customer-facing chatbot as project one. Start with drafts a person sends, and earn the right to automate.

Watch for the pet project, too: if the owner's favorite idea scored 30, it waits like everything else. And don't buy a new tool first; prove the workflow with the assistant you already have, then decide what to buy.

Never skip the baseline, because if you don't measure before, you can't prove after. And avoid side-of-desk projects by giving each owner protected time, even just two hours a week.

### 8. Do this now · 4:30

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Do this now: choose three, write the briefs, measure the baseline"]**

Choose your three projects using the portfolio and the filters, then write a brief for each. The full brief template is in the written lesson.

This week, before changing anything, measure the baseline for all three. Then put a 30-minute review in your calendar four weeks from today; Module 6 shows you exactly what to do in that meeting.

### 9. Recap and what's next · 4:55

**[ON CAMERA]**

Start with three projects: a quick win, a core workflow and a growth bet. Every project needs an owner, a baseline, digital inputs, a review rule and cheap failure. And earn the right to automate by beginning with AI drafts that a person reviews and sends.

In the next lesson, The Business Brief: Prompts That Produce Usable Work, you'll learn to brief AI the way you'd delegate to a capable new hire.
