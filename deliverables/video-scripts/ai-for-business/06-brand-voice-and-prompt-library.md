# Lesson 6: Your Brand Voice Guide and Team Prompt Library

| | |
|---|---|
| **Course** | AI for Business Owners: The Implementation Playbook |
| **Module** | 2 · Prompting for Business |
| **Length** | 5–6 min (~730 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Viewers can draft a one-page brand voice guide with AI's help and set up a team prompt library with a consistent entry format and simple rules that keep it usable.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat.
- Gather five to ten writing samples that sound right for a business: emails, web copy, social posts. Use your own, or clearly hypothetical samples, such as ones written for the neighborhood bakery example in the written lesson.
- Copy the voice-extraction prompt from Part 1 of the written lesson, ready to paste.
- Optional: a shared document set up with the six workflow folders, to show the library structure.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

Once a team starts using AI, two complaints show up almost immediately. "It doesn't sound like us." And "everyone gets different results."

Both have the same fix: shared assets. In this lesson, you'll build the two that matter most, a brand voice guide and a team prompt library.

### 2. Why AI sounds average · 0:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Gather · Extract · Edit · Test"]**

AI writes in an average voice by default, because it has learned from an enormous range of writing. To get your voice, you have to describe it explicitly and show examples.

Building the guide takes four steps: gather, extract, edit and test. Start by gathering five to ten pieces of writing you're proud of, like emails, web pages, social posts, proposals, or even a great text to a customer.

Mix authors and formats if you can, as long as each piece sounds right.

### 3. Demo: extract your voice · 0:55

Next, let AI do the extracting.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Below are samples of writing from my business that represent how we want to sound. Analyze them and create a brand voice guide with these sections:

1. Who we are (one paragraph)
2. Who we're talking to
3. Our voice in four traits, each written as "this, not that" (for example, "confident, not arrogant")
4. Words and phrases we use, and words we never use
5. Sentence style: length, contractions, jargon, emojis, exclamation marks
6. Formatting habits: headings, bullet points, greetings and sign-offs
7. Two short before-and-after examples that rewrite generic copy into our voice

Base everything on the samples. Don't invent claims about the business.

SAMPLES (separated by ---):
"""
[PASTE 5-10 SAMPLES]
"""
~~~

I'll paste the samples at the bottom, separated by three dashes. The prompt asks for seven sections, including four voice traits written as "this, not that," like confident, not arrogant.

It also asks for two short before-and-after examples that rewrite generic copy into your voice. And notice the last instruction: base everything on the samples, and don't invent claims about the business. What comes back is a solid first draft.

### 4. Edit, test and store it · 1:45

**[ON CAMERA]**

That draft is a good start, not a finished guide. Edit it by hand and add what only you know: phrases that grate on you, claims you legally can't make, and overused AI phrases you never want to see, like "unlock," "elevate" and "game-changer."

Then test it. Ask AI to rewrite three real pieces of content using the guide, and wherever the result doesn't sound like you, add that example to the guide as a "not this."

In the written lesson's hypothetical neighborhood bakery, one trait reads "friendly, not cutesy." They say "fresh this morning" and "see you Saturday," and they never say "indulge" or "elevate your mornings."

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Short enough to paste into any prompt: about one page"]**

A good voice guide is short enough to paste into any prompt, usually one page. Keep the master copy in a shared document with an owner and a last-updated date.

Then make it effortless to use. Most assistants let you save reusable instructions and reference files in a workspace, and business plans usually let you share them with your team.

### 5. The team prompt library · 2:55

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Name · Use when · Owner · Inputs needed · Prompt · Review rule · Data rule · Last tested"]**

Now the second asset. A prompt library is a shared collection of tested prompts for your recurring tasks. It turns one person's skill into a team capability, makes onboarding easier and makes quality consistent.

Every entry gets the same fields, and the written lesson lists them. There's a name, when to use it, an owner, the inputs needed, and the full prompt itself.

Then there's a review rule for what a person must check, a data rule, and the date and tool it was last tested on.

### 6. Keep the library usable · 3:35

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Three good results before a prompt joins the library"]**

A few conventions keep it usable. Organize by workflow, not by person, with folders like Marketing, Sales, Customer Service, Operations, People and Leadership.

Put anything that changes in square brackets and capitals, so people only edit what's in brackets. And set a quality bar: a prompt joins the library only after it has produced good results at least three times.

Review the library monthly; fifteen minutes is enough to retire prompts nobody uses, fix ones that drift and add new winners. Re-test your top ten when your assistant changes models or features. And when a prompt saves real time, note it next to the entry, because you'll use those wins to measure ROI in Module 6.

### 7. Do this now · 4:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Do this now: draft your voice guide, start your library"]**

Your exercise has two parts. First, draft your brand voice guide using the four steps. Give it 30 minutes; you'll refine it as you go.

Second, create your prompt library in a shared document or wiki, with the six workflow folders and the entry format from the written lesson. Add the prompt you built in the last lesson as entry number one.

### 8. Recap and what's next · 4:50

**[ON CAMERA]**

AI defaults to an average voice, and a one-page guide with examples makes it sound like you. Store it where it's effortless to use. A prompt library turns individual skill into team capability, with a consistent format, bracketed placeholders, a quality bar and a monthly review.

Every library entry has a data rule: green, amber or red. In the next lesson, Give AI Your Business Context, Safely, you'll learn exactly what those mean, and how to give AI the context it needs without putting customer trust at risk.
