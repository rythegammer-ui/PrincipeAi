# Lesson 7: Give AI Your Business Context, Safely

**Goal of this video:** Viewers can write a one-page business context pack using public-safe information and apply the Green, Amber and Red data rules, including anonymizing before they paste.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat, or a blank document if you'd rather fill in the template there.
- Copy the business context pack template from the written lesson, ready to paste.
- Prepare public-safe details to fill it in, for your own business or a clearly hypothetical one. Use only information you'd be comfortable publishing on a website.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

AI output gets dramatically better when the AI understands your business: what you sell, who you serve, how you work and what you never promise.

But not every piece of information belongs in every tool. This lesson shows you how to give AI the context it needs without putting customer trust or confidential information at risk.

### 2. Your business context pack · 0:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Business context pack: one page, updated quarterly"]**

Start with a business context pack. It's a one-page document you attach, paste or save into your assistant's workspace, so every task starts with the AI already knowing the basics. Write it once, and update it quarterly.

Here's the key rule. Keep the pack to information you'd be comfortable publishing on your website. That way it's safe to use in any approved tool, and you never have to think twice about it.

### 3. Demo: fill in the template · 0:55

Here's the template from the written lesson.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this template:

~~~
BUSINESS CONTEXT PACK - [COMPANY NAME] - updated [DATE]

What we do: [one or two sentences]
Who we serve: [ideal customers, industries, locations]
Our offers: [main services or products, who each is for, public price ranges]
What makes us different: [three honest points]
How we work: [the journey from first contact to delivery]
Common customer questions: [top five, with our standard answers]
Tone: [see our brand voice guide]
Things we never do or promise: [for example, guaranteed results, legal or financial advice]
Key terms: [industry or internal jargon, with meanings]
~~~

I'll work down the list: what we do in one or two sentences, who we serve, and our offers, with public price ranges only. Notice that the tone line simply points to the brand voice guide from the last lesson.

Pay attention to "things we never do or promise," like guaranteed results or legal or financial advice. What you never promise is part of the context the AI needs, just like what you sell.

The common customer questions, with your standard answers, are worth the effort too. Once they're in the pack, every task starts with them.

### 4. The traffic-light data rules · 1:55

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Green: public · Amber: internal · Red: restricted"]**

Most data mistakes happen because people don't have a simple rule to follow. So here's one that's easy to remember.

Green is public: website content, published prices, marketing materials and general industry questions. It's OK in any approved AI tool.

Amber is internal: internal processes, unpublished pricing, non-personal business data and anonymized customer scenarios. It goes only in business-grade tools your company has approved, with training on your data turned off.

Red is restricted: personal data about customers or staff, health or financial details, payment details, passwords, legal matters, and anything under a confidentiality agreement. It never goes in a general AI assistant, unless the business has approved a specific tool and process for it in writing.

### 5. Know your tool's settings · 2:45

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Training · Retention · Admin controls · Location · Connections"]**

Before Amber information goes into any tool, find out how it handles training, retention, admin controls, data location and connections. Is your data used to improve the provider's models, and can you switch that off? Consumer and business plans often differ here.

And if you connect email, files or your CRM, does the AI get access to more than you intended? Providers change their terms and settings, so check the current version yourself rather than relying on what a colleague heard.

### 6. Anonymize before you paste · 3:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Anonymize before you paste"]**

A mistake to avoid is pasting raw data and asking AI to clean it up, because by then it has already been shared. Anonymize before you paste instead. Replace names with roles, remove contact details and account numbers, and round figures when exact values don't matter.

So instead of pasting a complaint with a customer's full name, address, invoice number and card details, describe it. A residential customer was charged twice for the same invoice and is frustrated that she had to call twice. The AI can write an excellent reply, and nothing sensitive left your hands.

One more safeguard. When AI reads outside content, like incoming emails or web pages, that text can include instructions designed to steer it, sometimes called prompt injection. So keep a human approval step before any AI action that sends, deletes, pays for or shares anything.

### 7. Do this now · 4:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Do this now: write your context pack, list your AI tools"]**

Your exercise has two steps. First, write your context pack using the template in the written lesson, sticking to Green information, and save it where your team can reach it.

Second, list every AI tool your team uses right now. Ask people directly, including about personal accounts, and make it safe to answer honestly. For each tool, note the plan type and the training setting, because you'll use this list when you write your AI usage policy.

### 8. Recap and what's next · 4:55

**[ON CAMERA]**

A one-page context pack of public-safe facts makes every AI task better. Green is public, Amber goes only in approved business tools, and Red stays out unless explicitly approved. Anonymize before you paste, and keep a human approval step on any AI action that affects the outside world.
