# Lesson 8: The Content Engine: One Idea, Many Assets

| | |
|---|---|
| **Course** | AI for Business Owners: The Implementation Playbook |

| **Length** | 5–6 min (~730 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Viewers can run the five-step content engine, turning one recorded idea into a pillar piece and a set of derivatives, and apply the human edit pass before anything is published.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat.
- Prepare a short transcript of a voice memo answering a common customer question, plus a brand voice guide. Use your own, or clearly hypothetical ones, such as the heating and cooling example in the written lesson.
- Copy the pillar prompt and the derivatives prompt from the written lesson, ready to paste.
- Have the pillar article from the first demo ready to paste into the second prompt, in case you record the demos separately.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

Most owners know they should publish consistently. Very few have the time.

AI won't give you something worth saying; that part has to come from you. But it's exceptionally good at reshaping one strong idea into many formats.

That's the content engine. You supply the thinking once, AI handles the reshaping, and you edit before anything goes out.

### 2. Why AI-only content falls flat · 0:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Your thinking in. Many formats out."]**

If you ask AI to "write a blog post about kitchen renovations," you'll get the same generic article everyone else gets. It has no point of view, no experience behind it, and nothing a customer couldn't find anywhere else. Readers notice, and so do search engines.

The fix is to feed the engine raw material only you have. Think of the questions customers ask you on calls and in emails, and the mistakes you see people make before they hire you.

Add your opinions, including where you disagree with common advice in your industry. Then add anonymized stories from your work, and behind-the-scenes details about how you do things and why.

### 3. The engine in five steps · 1:10

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Capture · Transcribe · Pillar · Repurpose · Edit"]**

Here's the engine in five steps. One, capture: spend ten minutes recording a voice memo that answers one question your customers often ask, as if you're explaining it to a client across the table. Two, transcribe it; most phones, meeting tools and AI assistants can turn audio into text.

Three, AI turns your transcript into a pillar: one long-form piece in your voice, like a blog post or newsletter. Four, AI repurposes the pillar into smaller pieces. And five, a person edits every piece, then schedules it.

From one pillar, a realistic set is a newsletter, three social posts, two short video scripts, a carousel and an FAQ entry or two.

Then the derivatives prompt takes the finished article and creates the smaller pieces.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
From the article below, create:
1. Three social posts for [PLATFORM], each under [150] words, each with a different hook: a short story, a practical tip, and a common myth we disagree with.
2. Two 45-second video scripts, each making one point, written to be spoken naturally.
3. A newsletter introduction of about 120 words that links to the full article.
4. Two FAQ entries (question plus a 60-word answer) for our website.

Three social posts, each with a different hook, two short video scripts, a newsletter intro and two FAQ entries. The hooks differ on purpose: a short story, a practical tip, and a common myth you disagree with.

There are at most three hashtags per post, and no emojis unless your voice guide allows them. And again, no claims that aren't in the article.

### 5. The human edit pass · 3:25

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Never skip the edit pass"]**

Now the step you can never skip. The human edit pass is where AI content becomes your content.

Read it aloud: does it sound like us? Check that every fact, number and claim is true, and ours, and that every ADD EXAMPLE and CHECK marker is resolved.

Cut the filler and AI clichés, and keep one clear call to action. Make sure nothing reveals confidential client details, and that you have the rights to every image and quote you use.

### 6. A monthly rhythm · 4:00

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Week 1 record · Week 2 edit and schedule · Weeks 3–4 publish and learn"]**

A simple routine beats bursts of enthusiasm. In week one, record four voice memos and build the pillars. In week two, generate the derivatives, run the edit pass and schedule everything.

In weeks three and four, publish, reply to comments and note which topics got the best response. Budget roughly half a day a month to start, then adjust once you see what your team can sustain.

Picture a hypothetical family-run heating and cooling company. In October, the owner records ten minutes on five signs your furnace needs attention before winter. That becomes a blog post, a newsletter, social posts, short videos and new FAQs: one recording, one month of seasonal content in the owner's voice.

### 7. Do this now · 4:50

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Do this now: one voice memo, one pillar, publish one piece"]**

Record a five to ten minute voice memo answering the question your customers ask most often. Talk naturally, as if you're explaining it to a client across the table. Then transcribe it, run the pillar prompt, then the derivatives prompt.

Edit everything with the checklist in the written lesson, and publish at least one piece this week.

### 8. Recap and what's next · 5:15

**[ON CAMERA]**

You supply the ideas and experience; AI reshapes them into many formats. Feed the engine with customer questions, opinions and stories only you have. And the human edit pass is non-negotiable: voice, facts, markers, clichés and rights.

Your pillar article is just the start. In the next lesson, SEO and Website Copy That Converts, you'll use AI to make your website clearer, more persuasive and easier to find.
