# Lesson 9: SEO and Website Copy That Converts

**Goal of this video:** Viewers can use AI to audit and rewrite a service page with a proven structure, while relying on real data for SEO decisions and never letting AI invent proof.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat.
- Copy the text of one service page to audit, from your own site or a clearly hypothetical business, plus a short list of other page URLs for the internal-link question.
- Have your context pack and voice guide ready to paste into the service page prompt.
- Copy the audit prompt and the service page prompt from the written lesson, ready to paste.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

Your website works around the clock as either your best salesperson or your weakest. AI can help you rewrite pages so they're clearer, more persuasive and easier to find in search.

That works as long as you use it for the right jobs and keep the expertise yours. In this lesson, you'll audit a page and rewrite it, live.

### 2. What AI can and can't do for SEO · 0:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Real data for decisions. AI for thinking and writing."]**

AI is great at brainstorming the questions customers ask, and at structuring and drafting service pages, FAQs and articles. It can write title tags and meta descriptions within length limits, audit a page for clarity and weak calls to action, and suggest internal links.

What it can't reliably do is tell you real search volumes or your rankings, unless it's connected to an SEO data source, and even then, check the source. It doesn't know your local competition or what your customers actually type. And it can't add first-hand experience or proof you haven't given it.

So use real data for decisions: Google Search Console, your SEO tool, and the words customers use in their emails. Use AI for thinking and writing.

### 3. What search engines reward · 1:20

Google's published guidance focuses on whether content is helpful and made for people, not on whether AI helped write it. Mass-producing pages mainly to manipulate rankings is treated as spam.

The winning approach is the one that has always worked: useful pages with specifics only you can provide.

### 4. Map customer questions to pages · 1:40

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Problem-aware · Solution-aware · Ready to buy"]**

Before you rewrite anything, the written lesson has a prompt that maps customer questions to pages. It lists what a potential customer might search for or ask an AI assistant at three stages: problem-aware, solution-aware and ready to buy. Then it suggests whether each question belongs on a service page, in an FAQ or in a blog article.

It's told not to estimate search volumes. So sanity-check the list against your Search Console queries, the questions in your inbox and the objections from sales calls. Keep the questions real customers ask.

### 5. Demo: audit an existing page · 2:15

Let's start with a page you already have.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Here is the text of a page on my website. It's meant for: [IDEAL CUSTOMER].
Audit it as a conversion copywriter and an SEO editor:
1. Within five seconds, would a reader know what we do, who it's for and what to do next?
2. What questions would a buyer still have after reading it?
3. Point out vague claims, jargon and filler.
4. Suggest three better headlines and a stronger call to action.
5. Recommend three internal links to add, chosen from these pages: [LIST URLS].

PAGE TEXT:
"""
[PASTE]
"""
~~~

I'll paste the page text and list a few of my other pages for the internal links. The first question is the big one: within five seconds, would a reader know what we do, who it's for and what to do next?

The audit also lists the questions a buyer would still have, and points out vague claims, jargon and filler.

### 6. Rewrite with a proven structure · 3:00

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Headline · Subheadline · Problem · What's included · Proof · FAQs · Call to action"]**

Now rewrite the page with a structure that does much of the persuasion work for you. The headline states the outcome, who it's for and, for local businesses, where. Then come a subheadline, the problem in the reader's own words, what's included, proof, FAQs, and one clear call to action, repeated at the end.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Write a service page for [SERVICE] for [IDEAL CUSTOMER] in [LOCATION].
Structure: headline, subheadline, the problem, what's included, how it works (three to five steps), proof, five FAQs, call to action.

Use only these facts about us:
"""
[PASTE FROM YOUR CONTEXT PACK]
"""
In the proof section, write placeholders such as [INSERT REAL REVIEW] instead of inventing testimonials, numbers or credentials.

Main topic: [PHRASE]. Use it naturally in the headline, the first paragraph and one subheading; don't stuff it.
Also give me a title tag (under 60 characters) and a meta description (under 155 characters).

VOICE GUIDE:
"""
[PASTE]
"""
~~~

I'm pasting facts from my context pack and my voice guide. Notice the main topic instruction: use it naturally, and don't stuff it. You also get a title tag and a meta description within length limits.

### 7. Never let AI invent proof · 3:55

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Never let AI invent proof"]**

Here's the mistake that can really hurt you: letting AI invent proof. That's why the prompt asks for placeholders, like INSERT REAL REVIEW in brackets, instead of testimonials, numbers or credentials. Never publish invented reviews, statistics or credentials; insert the real thing.

Before you publish, verify facts, prices and claims, and read the page out loud. Make sure it includes something only you could say, like an example, a photo or a process detail.

For local businesses, keep your business name, address and phone number identical everywhere you're listed. Use AI to draft review responses, then personalize each one.

And create location pages only for areas you genuinely serve, with something specific to say about each. Pages that just swap the town name are thin content that hurts more than it helps.

### 8. Do this now · 4:50

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Do this now: audit, rewrite and publish one service page"]**

Pick your most important service page. Run the audit prompt on it, then rewrite it with the service page prompt and your context pack.

Edit the result and publish it. Then note the date, so you can compare inquiries from that page before and after.

### 9. Recap and what's next · 5:10

**[ON CAMERA]**

Use real search data for decisions, and AI for structuring, drafting and auditing. Helpful, specific, experience-rich pages win, and mass-produced filler doesn't. A clear structure does much of the persuasion work, and AI never gets to invent your proof.
