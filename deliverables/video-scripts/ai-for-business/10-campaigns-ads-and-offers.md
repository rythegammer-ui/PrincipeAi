# Lesson 10: Email, Social and Ad Campaigns (With Human Review)

|---|---|

**Goal of this video:** Viewers can pressure-test an offer, plan and draft a campaign with AI, and run every piece through a human review before it goes live.

## Before you record

- Open your AI assistant in a new chat.
- Fill in the offer worksheet from the written lesson for one promotion, your own or a clearly hypothetical one, and write a one-line description of the target customer.
- Copy the pressure-test prompt and the ad copy prompt from the written lesson, and decide what goes in the brackets.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

A campaign is a coordinated push toward one goal: filling a workshop, launching a service, promoting a seasonal offer or winning back past customers. Planning and writing one used to take days.

AI compresses the drafting to an hour or two. That leaves your time for the parts that decide results: the offer and the review.

In this lesson, you'll fix the offer first, then plan, draft and review the whole campaign.

### 2. Start with the offer, not the copy · 0:30

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Fix the offer before you write the copy"]**

Great copy can't rescue a weak offer. So before writing anything, fill in the offer worksheet in the written lesson: who it's for, the problem it solves, what they get, price and terms, and the call to action.

Two rows deserve extra care. "Why now" needs an honest reason, like a season, a real deadline or limited capacity. And "risk reversal" is a guarantee, trial or promise you can genuinely keep.

### 3. Demo: pressure-test the offer · 1:00

Then let AI pressure-test it before you write a single email.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Here is an offer I'm planning:
"""
[PASTE WORKSHEET]
"""
Act as my target customer: [DESCRIBE THEM]. Tell me honestly:
1. What would make you say yes right away?
2. What would make you hesitate or ignore it?
3. What would you need answered before buying?
Then suggest three ways to make the offer clearer or more compelling without discounting it.
~~~

I'll paste in the completed worksheet and describe the target customer. The AI answers as that customer: what would make them say yes, what would make them hesitate, and what they'd need answered before buying.

Then it suggests three ways to make the offer clearer or more compelling, without discounting it. Treat that as input to sharpen your offer before any copy gets written.

### 4. Plan the campaign and the emails · 1:50

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Announcement · Problem · Proof and details · Objections · Last call"]**

Next, ask AI for a day-by-day campaign calendar, kept realistic for a small team. That prompt is in the written lesson.

For email, a simple launch sequence has five emails: an announcement, the problem, proof and details, objections, and a last call, but only if there's a real deadline. Ask for a subject line, preview text and a body of no more than 200 words with a single call to action, plus three alternative subject lines to test.

Follow the email rules where you operate. Broadly, only email people who agreed to hear from you or have a relationship the law recognizes, include a working unsubscribe link and the required business details, and keep subject lines honest.

The specifics vary by country, for example CAN-SPAM in the US, CASL in Canada, and GDPR together with the electronic marketing rules in the UK and the EU. So check the rules where you and your subscribers are.

### 5. Demo: ad variations to test · 2:55

AI is excellent at producing variations to test: different hooks, angles and audiences.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Write ad copy for [PLATFORM] promoting [OFFER] to [AUDIENCE].
Give me six variations across three angles: (a) the problem, (b) the outcome, (c) the main objection.
For each: primary text under [125] characters, a headline under [40] characters, and an image idea.

Rules: no claims we can't prove, no guaranteed results, no fake urgency, and no wording that asserts or implies the viewer's personal characteristics (for example, "Struggling with your debt?"). Follow [PLATFORM]'s advertising policies.
~~~

Six variations across three angles: the problem, the outcome and the main objection. Each one gets primary text and a headline within a character limit, plus an image idea.

Now look at the rules line, because it matters.

No claims we can't prove, no guaranteed results, no fake urgency, and no wording that implies the viewer's personal characteristics. Major ad platforms reject ads that call out sensitive personal attributes, such as health, finances or age.

Then run two or three variations on a small budget, and let real results, not the AI's opinion, pick the winner.

### 6. The human review step · 3:55

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "True claims · Real scarcity · No fake reviews · Tested links · Consent"]**

Before anything goes live, someone checks it against the review checklist in the written lesson. If you work alone, review it the next morning with fresh eyes.

Every claim must be true and provable, including words like "best," "fastest" and "guaranteed." Scarcity must be real, so deadlines and limited spots actually exist. And there are no invented testimonials, reviews or endorsements; in the US, the FTC's rule on fake reviews explicitly covers AI-generated ones.

Test every price, date, link and promo code. Check industry rules if you're in a regulated field, like health, finance, legal or real estate, and confirm email consent and unsubscribe requirements are met. And make sure it sounds like your brand.

### 7. Do this now · 4:45

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Do this now: worksheet, pressure-test, plan, first email, review"]**

Choose one upcoming promotion. Complete the offer worksheet, pressure-test it with the customer prompt, then generate the campaign plan and the first email.

Run everything through the review checklist before it goes out. And when the campaign ends, the written lesson has a prompt to learn from your results, one that only draws conclusions the numbers support.

### 8. Recap and what's next · 5:10

**[ON CAMERA]**

Fix the offer before you write the copy, and use AI to pressure-test it. Plan the whole campaign as a calendar, draft each piece with clear limits, and let real results choose the winners. And a human reviews every claim, deadline, testimonial and consent rule before launch.

That completes marketing. In the next lesson, Respond to Leads Fast and Follow Up Consistently, we move into sales, starting with how you respond to the people who reach out.
