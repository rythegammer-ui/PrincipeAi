# Lesson 11: Respond to Leads Fast and Follow Up Consistently

| | |
|---|---|

| **Module** | 4 · Sales & Customer Service |
| **Length** | 5–6 min (~730 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Use AI to draft fast, specific replies to new inquiries and run a written follow-up cadence, with a person reviewing and sending every customer-facing message.

## Before you record

- Your AI assistant open in a new chat.
- One fictional or fully anonymized inquiry to paste into the reply prompt, plus a short, made-up services and FAQ summary. No real names or contact details on screen.
- A short fictional call note or CRM summary for the follow-up demo, and the post-quote cadence table from the written lesson ready to paste.
- Both prompts from the written lesson copied into a text file so you can paste them cleanly.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Lesson 11 · Respond to Leads Fast and Follow Up Consistently"]**

For many small businesses, the cheapest new revenue comes from leads you already have. Inquiries get answered late. Quotes never get followed up, and the "not right now" prospects are never contacted again.

That's not a marketing problem. It's a follow-up problem. And follow-up is mostly writing and remembering, which is exactly what AI and a little structure handle well.

**[OPTIONAL PERSONAL EXAMPLE: one sentence about a time a good lead went cold in your own business because nobody followed up.]**

### 2. Where leads leak · 0:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Slow replies · Generic replies · No quote follow-up · No system for 'not now' · Lost leads never contacted"]**

Start by sketching your lead journey from first inquiry to decision, then look for the gaps. The usual leaks are slow first replies, especially evenings, weekends and busy weeks, and generic replies that don't answer the question the person actually asked.

Then there's no follow-up after a quote, because everyone assumes the prospect will get in touch. There's no system for "not now" leads, who were interested but not ready. And lost leads and past customers are never contacted again.

### 3. Level 1: AI drafts the reply · 1:00

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Level 1: AI drafts, a person reviews and sends"]**

Level one is where you start. You combine your context pack, your FAQ answers and your voice guide with a reply prompt. AI drafts, and a person reviews and sends.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
You're drafting a first reply to a new inquiry for [COMPANY].
Our services, pricing guidance and FAQs:
"""
[PASTE OR ATTACH]
"""
Our goal for this first reply: [BOOK A CALL / BOOK A SITE VISIT / COMPLETE A QUOTE REQUEST FORM].

Inquiry:
"""
[PASTE]
"""

Write a reply that:
- Thanks them by name and shows we read their message by referencing one specific detail
- Answers their direct questions using only our information; if we can't answer something, say we'll confirm it on the call
- Asks at most two qualifying questions: [FOR EXAMPLE, TIMELINE, BUDGET RANGE, LOCATION]
- Ends with one clear next step and this link: [BOOKING LINK]
Keep it under 140 words.

Then, separately, write a one-line lead summary for our CRM: need, timeline, budget signal, fit (high, medium or low) and why.
~~~

Look at what this prompt insists on. The reply has to reference one specific detail from their message, answer only from our information, ask no more than two qualifying questions and end with one clear next step.

Before anything goes out, a person reads the draft. Did it answer only from our information? If there's something it couldn't answer, it should say we'll confirm it on the call, not guess.

Now look at the second part. Every lead arrives in your CRM with a consistent one-line summary of need, timeline, budget signal and fit. That's a quiet time-saver, because it makes pipeline reviews much faster.

### 4. Level 2: A written follow-up cadence · 2:15

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Day 0 · Day 2 · Day 6 · Day 12 · Day 25 · Then newsletter, with consent"]**

Level two is a follow-up system. You write your cadence down once, so follow-up stops depending on memory.

The written lesson gives you a starting point for after a quote. On day two you ask if they have questions and offer a 15-minute walkthrough. Day six adds value, day twelve is a direct, friendly check on whether it's still a priority, and day twenty-five politely closes the loop.

Adjust the timing to your sales cycle. Then let AI write the messages.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Write a follow-up sequence for a prospect who received our quote for [SERVICE].
Context:
"""
[PASTE CALL NOTES OR CRM SUMMARY]
"""
Use this cadence: [PASTE CADENCE].
Each message: under 100 words, adds something useful, references their specific situation and ends with one low-pressure question. No guilt-tripping and no fake deadlines. The final message is a polite close-the-loop.
~~~

One rule makes follow-up work. Every message must reference something specific to that person.

A generic "just checking in" is noise. A message that mentions their timeline or their concern about disruption is service.

### 5. Don't automate too early · 3:15

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Automate the handoffs only after the manual version works"]**

The big mistake to avoid is automating before the manual version works. Run drafted replies and follow-ups by hand for a few weeks first. Then connect the handoffs with your CRM's built-in automation or a no-code platform such as Zapier or Make, so drafts land somewhere a team member edits and sends them.

Keep human approval on customer-facing messages until you have weeks of evidence the drafts are consistently right. Keep it permanently for anything involving price or scope. The one thing you can send instantly without AI is a simple, pre-written acknowledgment saying you got their message and when they'll hear back.

And every few months, reactivate past customers and "not now" leads. Describe each segment to the AI without pasting names or contact details, then send a useful check-in from a real person, only to people you have permission to contact.

### 6. Do this now · 4:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Track: median time to first reply · follow-up completion rate · quote-to-win rate"]**

Before you change anything, note three numbers so you can compare before and after: median time to first reply, the share of quotes that got the full cadence and your quote-to-win rate.

Then take your last ten inquiries, anonymized if they contain personal details. Run each one through the reply prompt and compare the drafts with what was actually sent. Finally, write your post-quote cadence and set it up as recurring tasks in your CRM or calendar, so follow-up happens whether or not anyone remembers.

### 7. Recap and what's next · 4:55

**[ON CAMERA]**

So here's the recap. Slow and missing follow-up often costs more revenue than a shortage of leads. Start with AI-drafted replies that a person sends, plus a one-line CRM summary.

Write your cadence down and make every follow-up specific to the person. Automate the handoffs only after the manual version works, and keep approval on anything about price.

Once a lead books that call, the conversation itself is where you win or lose the deal. In the next lesson, Sales Call Prep, Call Notes and Proposals, you'll use AI for the work around that conversation: the prep before, the notes after and a tailored proposal. See you there.
