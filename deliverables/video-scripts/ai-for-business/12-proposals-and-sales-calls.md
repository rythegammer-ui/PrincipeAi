# Lesson 12: Sales Call Prep, Call Notes and Proposals

| | |
|---|---|
| **Course** | AI for Business Owners: The Implementation Playbook |
| **Module** | 4 · Sales & Customer Service |
| **Length** | 5–6 min (~720 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Use AI to prepare for a sales call, turn the conversation into a structured summary and draft a tailored proposal from a reusable template, with a person entering and checking every price.

## Before you record

- Your AI assistant open in a new chat.
- A fictional prospect for the prep demo: a made-up inquiry, a couple of short emails and a one-paragraph summary of your offers. No real client names or details.
- For the proposal demo: your proposal template, two or three standard scope blocks and a short fictional call summary, ready to paste.
- Both prompts from the written lesson copied into a text file so you can paste them cleanly.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Lesson 12 · Sales Call Prep, Call Notes and Proposals"]**

Sales conversations are where your business is won or lost. AI can't have them for you, and it shouldn't.

What it can do is handle the work around them: the research before, the notes during and the proposal after. That's often hours per deal, and the quality goes up at the same time.

### 2. Before the call: a 10-minute prep brief · 0:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Before: prep brief · During: capture · After: summary and proposal"]**

Start by gathering what you already have: the inquiry, past emails, your CRM notes and anything public about their business. Then ask for a one-page call brief.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
I have a sales call with [NAME, ROLE] at [COMPANY] about [TOPIC].
What I know:
"""
[PASTE INQUIRY, EMAILS, CRM NOTES AND PUBLIC INFORMATION FROM THEIR WEBSITE]
"""
Our offers:
"""
[PASTE A SHORT SUMMARY OF YOUR OFFERS]
"""
Prepare a one-page call brief:
1. What they likely want and why now (label assumptions clearly as assumptions)
2. Five discovery questions about their goals, constraints, decision process and budget
3. The three objections most likely to come up, each with a short, honest response
4. Which of our offers fits best, and why
5. A suggested agenda for a 30-minute call
~~~

Notice that the first item asks it to label assumptions clearly. That way, anything the AI is guessing about their situation becomes something to test on the call, not something to repeat as fact.

If your assistant can browse the web, it can gather public information for you. Verify anything you plan to mention on the call, and never present a guess about their business as fact.

### 3. During the call: capture, don't type · 1:20

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Always ask permission before recording"]**

Typing notes while someone talks splits your attention. You have two better options.

One: record and transcribe. Many video-call tools and AI note-takers can do this, but always ask permission first. Recording laws vary, and some places require everyone on the call to consent.

Two: if you'd rather not record, jot a few words during the call. Then dictate a two-minute voice memo straight after, while it's fresh.

### 4. After the call: a structured summary · 1:45

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Use only what was said · Mark anything uncertain with CHECK"]**

Next, paste the transcript or your notes into the summary prompt from the written lesson. It pulls out their goals in their own words, their problems, decision makers, timeline, budget signals, objections and next steps, each with an owner and a date. It also writes a one-line note for your CRM.

The key instruction is "use only what was said," with anything uncertain marked for you to check. So before you rely on the summary, look for those marks and confirm each one.

Paste the CRM note in right away, and send the prospect a short recap of next steps the same day. Prospects notice when you remember exactly what they said.

### 5. Proposals: build the template once · 2:35

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Template + scope blocks + call summary = tailored proposal"]**

Much of any proposal is the same from one client to the next. So you build a template once, with sections for their situation and goals, your approach, scope, timeline, investment, why us and next steps. Then AI tailors it from your call summary.

Each section has a source. Their goals come from the call summary, in their words, and scope comes from your standard scope blocks, covering what's included and what's not. The investment comes from your price list, entered by a person, and "why us" uses real proof only.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Draft a proposal for [CLIENT] using our template and the call summary below.
Rules:
- Describe their goals in their own words.
- Choose scope only from our standard scope blocks; don't invent services.
- Leave every price as [PRICE]. I'll add pricing from our price list.
- Keep it under [TWO] pages, in plain English with no jargon.

TEMPLATE:
"""
[PASTE]
"""
SCOPE BLOCKS:
"""
[PASTE]
"""
CALL SUMMARY:
"""
[PASTE]
"""
~~~

Check what comes back. Their goals should be in their own words, the scope should come only from blocks you've already written, and every price should still be a placeholder.

### 6. The pricing rule, and a skeptical read · 3:40

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "AI never sets prices. A person enters and checks them."]**

That's deliberate. It's the pricing rule, and it isn't optional: AI never sets prices or calculates totals that go to a client unchecked. You insert prices from your own pricing sheet and double-check any arithmetic.

Before you send it, there's one more prompt in the written lesson worth using. You ask the AI to read your proposal as the client's most skeptical decision maker, who's comparing you with a cheaper option. Then you fix each doubt it raises, without adding claims you can't support.

### 7. Do this now · 4:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Build your template · Write your scope blocks · Time each step"]**

Here's your exercise. Build your proposal template and write your standard scope blocks.

Then use the prep, summary and proposal prompts on your next sales call, or retroactively on a recent one. Time each step, so you have a baseline to compare against.

And once a month, paste five to ten anonymized call summaries, from deals won and lost, and ask AI for patterns: recurring objections, the questions you're asked every time and the reasons deals stall. Feed what you learn back into your FAQs, website copy and offers.

### 8. Recap and what's next · 4:55

**[ON CAMERA]**

Let's recap. AI handles the work around the sales conversation: prep, notes and proposals. Always get consent before recording, and verify any research before you use it.

A reusable template plus a call summary produces tailored proposals quickly. And AI never sets prices; a person enters and checks them every time.

Winning the customer is only half the job; looking after them is the other half. In the next lesson, Customer Support: Drafts, FAQ Assistants and Escalation, you'll see how AI can help you answer customers faster, and exactly when a person needs to take over.
