# Lesson 14: Turn Know-How into SOPs and Meetings into Action Items

| | |
|---|---|
| **Course** | AI for Business Owners: The Implementation Playbook |
| **Module** | 5 · Operations & Admin |
| **Length** | 5–6 min (~720 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Turn a recorded walkthrough into a tested SOP with the record, transcribe, structure method, and turn any meeting transcript into decisions and owned action items.

## Before you record

- Your AI assistant open in a new chat.
- A short transcript of someone explaining a routine task. Use a fictional one, or a real one with names and customer details removed.
- A short fictional meeting transcript that includes at least one action with no owner or date, so the flags show up in the demo.
- Both prompts from the written lesson copied into a text file so you can paste them cleanly.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Lesson 14 · Turn Know-How into SOPs and Meetings into Action Items"]**

Two quiet drains slow down almost every growing business. Knowledge that lives only in someone's head, and meetings that end without clear next steps.

Both are documentation problems. And documentation is where AI shines, because it can turn messy speech into clean structure in seconds.

### 2. Record, transcribe, structure · 0:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Record · Transcribe · Structure · Test · Publish"]**

Standard operating procedures make work consistent, make delegation possible and protect you when a key person is away. Most businesses don't have them because writing them is tedious. The record, transcribe, structure method removes the tedium.

First, the person who does the task best records themselves doing it. That's a screen recording with narration for computer work, or a phone video or voice memo for physical work. No script; they talk as if they're training a new hire, and explain why as well as what.

Then you transcribe the recording with your phone, your meeting tool or your AI assistant. And then AI turns the transcript into an SOP using a standard format.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Turn this transcript of [NAME OR ROLE] explaining how they [TASK] into a standard operating procedure.

Format:
- Title, purpose (one sentence), owner, last reviewed [DATE]
- When to use this SOP (the trigger)
- What you need before starting (tools, access, inputs)
- Numbered steps: one action per step, each starting with a verb. Include the "why" for any step that's easy to skip.
- Quality checks: how to know it was done right
- Common mistakes and how to avoid them
- What to do if something goes wrong, and who to ask

Keep the expert's tips. Don't add steps they didn't describe. If a step seems to be missing, list it under "Questions for the owner" at the end.

TRANSCRIPT:
"""
[PASTE]
"""
~~~

If it spotted a gap, you'll find it at the end of the SOP. That "Questions for the owner" section is the secret weapon. It catches the steps experts skip because they've done them a thousand times, and the AI asks about them instead of inventing them.

### 3. Test it, then publish it · 1:45

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Every hesitation becomes an edit"]**

Now test it. Someone who doesn't usually do the task follows the SOP while the expert watches silently. Every point where they hesitate becomes an edit.

Then publish it in a shared place, with an owner and a review date. Which SOPs first? Use your task inventory from Lesson 2, and prioritize tasks only one person knows how to do, tasks new hires often get wrong, high-stakes tasks and tasks you want to delegate soon.

There's a bonus, too. Once your SOPs are written, they become context for AI. In a business-grade tool your company has approved, a shared workspace loaded with your SOPs can answer "how do we" questions for your team.

### 4. Meetings into action items · 2:30

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Get consent · Check where it's stored · Keep sensitive meetings off AI note-takers"]**

Now, meetings. Many video-call platforms and AI note-takers can record and transcribe meetings automatically. Before you switch one on, tell attendees when a meeting is recorded and get their agreement, because recording laws vary by location.

Check where recordings and transcripts are stored and who can access them. And decide which meetings are never recorded by an AI note-taker, such as HR conversations, performance reviews and legal discussions. Then use one prompt for every meeting.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
From the meeting transcript below, produce:
1. Decisions made, one line each
2. Action items as a table: action, owner, due date, status. If no owner or date was agreed, write "UNASSIGNED" or "NO DATE"; don't guess.
3. Open questions and unresolved disagreements
4. A five-line summary for people who missed the meeting

TRANSCRIPT:
"""
[PASTE]
"""
~~~

Here's the table of action items. Where no owner or date was agreed, you'll see the flag.

Those UNASSIGNED and NO DATE flags are the point. They show you, in black and white, where meetings end without commitment. After a few weeks, people start assigning owners during the meeting, just to avoid the flag.

### 5. Close the loop · 3:40

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Review · Into your task manager · Recap within the hour · Start with last week's actions"]**

Don't send the AI summary straight out. The meeting owner reviews it first, because AI can mishear names and numbers and misattribute who said what.

Then the action items go straight into your task manager or CRM, and the recap goes out within an hour, while the meeting is still fresh. The next meeting starts by reviewing the last meeting's action items.

You can use AI before the meeting, too. Paste your list of topics and ask for a 30-minute agenda with a desired outcome for each item. Meetings with a stated outcome tend to end with decisions instead of discussions.

### 6. Do this now · 4:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "One task · One walkthrough · One tested SOP"]**

Here's your exercise. Pick one task only one person knows how to do. Have them record a ten-minute walkthrough, turn it into an SOP with the prompt, and test it with someone else.

Then, at your next team meeting, use the summary prompt and send the recap within the hour. The written lesson has a short meeting checklist to keep next to you: a stated purpose and outcome, consent or no recording, an owner review before sending, and an owner and a date for every action item.

### 7. Recap and what's next · 5:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

Let's recap. Record, transcribe, structure and test is the fastest route to SOPs people actually use. Ask AI to list missing steps as questions rather than invent them.

Get consent before recording, and keep sensitive meetings off AI note-takers. And flag unassigned actions and missing dates; they show where meetings fail to produce commitment.
