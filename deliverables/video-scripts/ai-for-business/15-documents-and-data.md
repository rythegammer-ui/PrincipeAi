# Lesson 15: Documents, Spreadsheets and Reports (With Verification)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Course** | AI for Business Owners: The Implementation Playbook |
| **Module** | 5 · Operations & Admin |
| **Length** | 5–6 min (~710 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Use AI to compare documents and analyze spreadsheets quickly, and apply the "show me where" rule and five verification checks before any result reaches a decision.

## Before you record

- Your AI assistant open in a new chat, ideally one that can analyze an uploaded file by running real calculations.
- Two or three sample supplier quotes with fictional company names, saved as files you can attach.
- A small sample spreadsheet with made-up figures, such as twelve months of sales by service, with no customer names or emails. Know one or two totals in advance so you can check the AI on camera.
- Both prompts from the written lesson copied into a text file so you can paste them cleanly.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Lesson 15 · Documents, Spreadsheets and Reports (With Verification)"]**

Every business runs on documents and numbers: contracts, supplier quotes, policies, reports and spreadsheets. AI can read faster than any person and explain patterns in plain English.

It can also misread a column, miscalculate a total or state something that isn't in the document, with complete confidence. This lesson gives you the speed without the risk.

### 2. Documents: compare and extract · 0:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Summarize · Compare · Extract"]**

High-value uses include summarizing a long contract or policy, comparing several supplier quotes side by side, extracting key details from forms and invoices, and drafting a reply to a formal letter. Let's compare some quotes.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Compare the attached supplier quotes for [WHAT WE'RE BUYING].
Create a table with: supplier, total price, what's included, what's excluded, delivery time, payment terms, warranty, contract length and cancellation terms.
Quote the exact wording of any term that could cost us money later.
Write "NOT STATED" for anything a quote doesn't mention; don't assume.
Then list five questions I should ask each supplier before deciding.
~~~

Two instructions do the heavy lifting here. Any term that could cost you money later is quoted word for word, and anything a quote doesn't mention says "NOT STATED" instead of a guess.

For contracts, the written lesson has a similar prompt that explains the contract in plain English, flags unusual or one-sided clauses with the exact wording, and writes questions for your lawyer. It's an excellent way to understand what you're reading and prepare better questions. It does not replace a lawyer for anything important.

### 3. The "show me where" rule · 1:30

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Show me where: cite the section, quote the words, spot-check the original"]**

Here's a rule worth adopting today. For every important claim, ask the AI to cite the section or quote the exact words. Then spot-check those against the original.

In practice, that means asking which section a clause comes from, or for the exact sentence behind a point in the summary, then opening the original to find it yourself. If it can't point to the source, treat the claim as unverified.

### 4. Spreadsheets: confirm the data first · 2:00

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Use tools that run real calculations"]**

For numbers, use an assistant that can analyze an uploaded file by writing and running real calculations. That's far more reliable than an AI doing math in its head in a chat reply. If your tool doesn't show that it ran a calculation, treat its numbers as estimates to verify.

Prepare the data first: one table per sheet, one header row and clear column names.

Remove personal data you don't need, like customer names and emails, following your traffic-light rules. And note what each column means and any quirks, such as refunds showing as negative amounts.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Attached is [DESCRIBE THE DATA, e.g., 12 months of sales by service and month]. Columns: [EXPLAIN EACH ONE].
1. First, describe the data back to me: number of rows, date range, and any missing or odd values. Then stop and wait for me to confirm.
2. After I confirm, calculate: [SPECIFIC QUESTIONS, e.g., revenue by service per quarter, average job value by month].
3. Show the calculations or code you used.
4. Summarize the three most important findings in plain English, and for each one say what else could explain it.
~~~

Check that it stops and describes the data back before calculating anything. Step one is the most important line in that prompt. It catches a misread column before every answer built on it is wrong.

### 5. The five verification checks · 3:10

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Totals reconcile · Spot-check three rows · Sense check · Source check · Re-run"]**

The mistake to avoid is trusting a confident number. So run the five checks from the written lesson.

Do the totals reconcile with your accounting system, or a simple SUM in the spreadsheet? Pick three random rows and calculate them by hand.

Then sense-check it: does the result fit what you know about your business? If it's surprising, verify twice. Trace every quote and figure back to the original, and re-run the question in a fresh conversation; different answers mean dig deeper.

### 6. Reports and formulas · 3:45

**[ON CAMERA]**

For monthly reports, let your accounting software, CRM or spreadsheet produce the numbers, and let AI write the commentary around them. The report prompt in the written lesson uses only the numbers you provide, labels possible reasons as hypotheses, and flags anything that doesn't add up instead of recalculating.

AI is also excellent at writing and explaining spreadsheet formulas. Describe what you need in plain English, such as a formula that sums one column only where another column says "Repair" and the date is in the current month. Then test the formula on a few rows where you already know the answer.

### 7. Do this now · 4:30

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "One monthly report · Describe it back · All five checks"]**

Here's your exercise. Pick a report or spreadsheet you review every month. Run it through the analysis prompt, starting with the "describe it back to me" step.

Then work through all five verification checks. Note anything the checks caught; it will tell you how much review this kind of task needs.

### 8. Recap and what's next · 4:55

**[ON CAMERA]**

Let's recap. AI reads and summarizes documents quickly, but every important claim needs a source you can check. Use tools that run real calculations for data work, and confirm the AI understood the data first.

Reconcile totals, spot-check rows, sense-check results, trace sources and re-run when in doubt. And for contracts and other high-stakes documents, AI prepares your questions; a professional answers them.
