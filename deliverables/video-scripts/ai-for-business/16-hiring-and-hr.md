# Lesson 16: Hiring and HR Drafts, Done Fairly

**Goal of this video:** Use AI to draft fair, job-related job ads and structured interview guides, while keeping every decision about a person with a person and keeping candidate and employee data out of unapproved tools.

## Before you record

- Your AI assistant open in a new chat.
- A fictional role to use in both demos, with a made-up business description, 90-day success list, must-have and nice-to-have skills, pay range, location and hours filled in.
- Keep real candidates, CVs and employee details completely off screen.
- Both prompts from the written lesson copied into a text file so you can paste them cleanly. Have the job ad from the first demo ready to paste into the second.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Lesson 16 · Hiring and HR Drafts, Done Fairly"]**

People work is full of writing: job ads, interview questions, offer letters, onboarding plans, handbooks and policies. AI can produce strong drafts of all of them in minutes.

But this is also where the stakes are highest, with employment law, discrimination risk and people's livelihoods involved. So the rule for this lesson is simple.

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "AI drafts documents. People make decisions about people."]**

AI drafts documents. People make decisions about people.

### 2. Where AI helps, and where it doesn't · 0:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Safe: job ads · interview guides · work samples · onboarding · first drafts of policies"]**

The safe, high-value uses are documents. Job ads that describe outcomes and what the role is really like. Structured interview guides with clear scoring criteria, realistic work-sample tasks, onboarding plans, training materials built from your SOPs, and first drafts of policies for review by a qualified professional.

Now the other list: use with great care, or not at all. That starts with screening, ranking or rejecting candidates with AI. Models can reflect biases in the data they learned from, and some places now regulate automated hiring tools.

New York City, for example, requires bias audits and candidate notices for certain automated employment decision tools. And the EU AI Act treats AI used in recruitment as high-risk.

The same caution goes for performance ratings, discipline or dismissal decisions, and anything touching health, disability, family status, religion or other protected characteristics. And don't upload CVs, personnel files or employee data into tools that aren't approved for personal data. That's Red data.

### 3. Write a better job ad · 1:35

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Outcomes, not just duties · Only genuine must-haves · Inclusive, plain language"]**

Let's draft a job ad.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Write a job ad for a [ROLE] at [COMPANY], a [ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION OF THE BUSINESS].
What success looks like in the first 90 days: [LIST]
Must-have skills: [ONLY GENUINE REQUIREMENTS]
Nice-to-have skills: [LIST]
Pay range: [RANGE]. Location and hybrid details: [DETAILS]. Hours: [DETAILS].

Rules:
- Use inclusive, plain language and avoid gendered wording and unnecessary jargon
- Don't require a degree or a number of years' experience unless I listed it as a must-have
- Keep the must-have list short so we don't put off good candidates
- Explain how to apply and what the hiring process looks like
~~~

Check that it leads with what success looks like in the first 90 days, not just a list of duties. The must-haves should stay short, with no degree or years-of-experience requirement unless you put one there.

It should also tell candidates how to apply and what the hiring process looks like. Read it the way a candidate would, and ask whether a good person who doesn't tick every box would still apply.

A growing number of places require pay ranges in job ads or early in the hiring process, so check your local rules. Even where it's optional, including the range saves everyone time.

### 4. A structured interview guide · 2:35

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Same core questions · Clear scoring guide · Score independently"]**

Next, we turn that job ad into a structured interview guide.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Create a structured interview guide for [ROLE] based on this job ad:
"""
[PASTE]
"""
- Six questions tied to the must-have skills: a mix of behavioral ("Tell me about a time...") and situational ("What would you do if...")
- For each question, describe what a strong, an average and a weak answer looks like, as a 1-5 scoring guide
- Questions must be job-related only. Nothing about age, family, health, religion, nationality or other personal characteristics.
- Suggest a short work-sample task for finalists that takes under 90 minutes
~~~

Each question should tie to a must-have skill, with a scoring guide for strong, average and weak answers. And every question should be job-related only.

Then use it consistently. Every candidate gets the same core questions, and interviewers score independently before comparing notes. That's fairer to candidates and gives you better information.

### 5. A quiet mistake, then onboarding and policies · 3:20

**[ON CAMERA]**

A mistake to avoid here is a quiet one: pasting a pile of CVs into a general chatbot and asking it to pick the best three. That puts Red data in the wrong place and hands a decision about people to AI.

Where AI does help is onboarding. The written lesson has a prompt for a 30-day plan, organized week by week, with a first-day checklist and the three things a new hire should be able to do independently by day 30.

For policies, AI is good at drafting the structure and plain-English wording, say for remote work or expenses. A qualified HR advisor or employment lawyer should review it before it goes into your handbook.

For difficult messages, like a schedule change or declining a request, AI can help you find clear, respectful words. But important conversations still happen in person. Employment law differs by country and state and is changing quickly, so when in doubt, check with an employment lawyer or a qualified HR advisor.

### 6. Do this now · 4:30

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Job ad + interview guide · Colleague review · Save to your People folder"]**

Here's your exercise. Choose the next role you'll hire for, or an existing job ad that needs a refresh. Run the job ad and interview guide prompts.

Then ask a colleague to review both for clarity, fairness and job-relatedness, and save both prompts to the People folder of your prompt library. Use the HR review checklist in the written lesson as your final check.

### 7. Recap and what's next · 4:55

**[ON CAMERA]**

Let's recap. AI drafts people documents; people make people decisions. Structured interviews with clear scoring guides are fairer and more useful.

Don't use general AI tools to screen or rank candidates, and keep employee data out of unapproved tools. Laws vary and change quickly, so get qualified review for policies and anything with legal weight.
