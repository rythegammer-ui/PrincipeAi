# Lesson 17: Write Your AI Usage Policy

| | |
|---|---|
| **Course** | AI for Business Owners: The Implementation Playbook |
| **Module** | 6 · Rollout, Safety & ROI |
| **Length** | 5–6 min (~740 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Fill in a short AI usage policy covering approved tools, data rules, human review, transparency and reporting mistakes, tailor it to your business and roll it out to your team.

## Before you record

- Your AI assistant open in a new chat.
- The policy template from the written lesson, ready to paste where the tailoring prompt asks for it.
- A fictional business to fill in the brackets for the demo: business type, number of employees, location, the kinds of data it handles and its approved tools.
- The tailoring prompt from the written lesson copied into a text file so you can paste it cleanly.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Lesson 17 · Write Your AI Usage Policy"]**

Your team is very likely using AI already. Possibly on personal accounts, and possibly with information that shouldn't be there.

The tempting response is to ban it. But banning AI rarely works; it just pushes use out of sight. A short, clear policy does the opposite: it gives people permission to use AI well, and firm lines they won't cross.

### 2. What a good policy does · 0:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Says yes clearly · Draws hard lines · Stays short · Names an owner"]**

A good AI policy does four things. It says yes clearly to the uses you want, so people stop guessing. It draws hard lines around data, decisions and customer-facing content.

It stays short, one to two pages, so people actually read it. And it names an owner and a review date, because tools and rules change.

### 3. Inside the template · 0:50

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Purpose · Approved tools · Data rules · Human review · Transparency · Access · IP · Mistakes · Review"]**

The written lesson gives you a complete template to copy. You fill in the brackets and cut anything that doesn't apply. It opens with a simple purpose: we use AI to save time, improve quality and serve customers better, and when in doubt, ask the owner.

Approved tools says which tool is approved for which kind of data. Personal or free accounts get Green data only, and any new AI tool needs the owner's approval before it touches company data.

Data rules are your traffic lights. Green is public information, and Amber is internal, non-personal business information, for approved business tools only.

Red covers personal data about customers or staff, health or financial details, payment details, passwords, legal matters and anything under a confidentiality agreement, and it never goes into AI tools without a specific, written approval. And the template asks everyone to anonymize before pasting: remove names, contact details and identifying numbers.

Human review means a person checks anything a customer, candidate or the public will see, and facts, numbers, prices, dates and links are checked against the source. AI never makes the final call on pricing, refunds above your threshold, hiring, discipline, credit or legal matters.

Transparency means any assistant talking to customers says it's AI, and you never publish AI-generated reviews or testimonials, or images presented as real people or events. And under accounts and access, any automation that sends, deletes, pays for or shares something needs a human approval step.

### 4. Section 8 matters more than it looks · 2:35

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "We fix problems; we don't blame people for reporting them."]**

Section 8 covers mistakes. If AI output causes a mistake, or someone thinks information went somewhere it shouldn't, they tell the owner the same day.

And the policy says it plainly: we fix problems; we don't blame people for reporting them. That line matters, because if people fear blame, small problems get hidden until they become big ones.

### 5. Tailor it to your business · 3:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Regulated industry? Client contracts? Get professional advice."]**

Next, adapt it. Healthcare, financial services, legal, insurance and work with children all carry extra requirements, so get professional advice for your sector. Some clients also restrict AI use on their work or data, so check your contracts and confidentiality agreements, and add a line if needed.

If your team is larger, add short role-specific guidance, such as what sales can and can't send without review.

You can use AI to do the first pass of tailoring.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Tailor this AI usage policy for a [BUSINESS TYPE] with [NUMBER] employees in [COUNTRY / STATE]. We handle these kinds of data: [LIST]. Our approved tools are: [LIST].
Keep it under two pages, in plain English. Flag any areas where I should get legal advice for my industry or location. Don't present anything as legal advice.

POLICY:
"""
[PASTE TEMPLATE]
"""
~~~

Check what comes back. It should stay under two pages and flag the areas where you should get legal advice for your industry or location. Those flags are your list of questions for a professional, not answers in themselves.

### 6. Roll it out properly · 4:05

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Finalize · Walk through · Acknowledge · Pin · Review"]**

Then roll it out properly. Finalize it, with professional review if you're in a regulated field. Then walk the team through it in 20 minutes, using real examples.

A quick round of "Is this Green, Amber or Red?" works well. Ask everyone to acknowledge it in writing, even just a reply to an email.

Then pin it wherever people work, link it from your prompt library, and review it on the date you set.

### 7. Do this now · 4:35

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Fill in the template · Name an owner · Book the 20-minute walkthrough"]**

Here's your exercise. Using the tool list you made in Lesson 7, fill in the template. Decide which tools are approved for which data, set your review rules and name an owner.

Then schedule the 20-minute team walkthrough for next week.

### 8. Recap and what's next · 4:55

**[ON CAMERA]**

Let's recap. A clear policy gives permission and guardrails; a ban just hides AI use. Cover approved tools, data rules, human review, transparency, access, IP and reporting mistakes.

Adapt it for your industry and client contracts, with professional advice where needed. Keep it short, walk the team through it and review it regularly.

A policy sets the rules, but people still need the confidence to use AI well. In the next lesson, Train Your Team and Win Over the Skeptics, you'll build a simple training plan that takes your team from wary to confident.
