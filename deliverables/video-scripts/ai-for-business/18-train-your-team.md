# Lesson 18: Train Your Team and Win Over the Skeptics

| | |
|---|---|

| **Module** | 6 · Rollout, Safety & ROI |
| **Length** | 5–6 min (~710 spoken words) |
| **Format** | Talking head + screen recording |

**Goal of this video:** Address your team's concerns honestly, run a four-session training plan on real work, appoint AI champions and draft the announcement that kicks it all off.

## Before you record

- Your AI assistant open in a new chat.
- A fictional role, business type and list of everyday tasks to fill in the practice-exercise prompt.
- For the announcement demo: a team size, the areas where you'll start using AI and the tone you want. Keep it generic; no real names.
- Both prompts from the written lesson copied into a text file so you can paste them cleanly.

## Script

### 1. Hook · 0:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Lesson 18 · Train Your Team and Win Over the Skeptics"]**

Tools don't create ROI; habits do. And most AI rollouts stall for human reasons, not technical ones.

People don't know where to start, or they worry about what AI means for their jobs. Or they tried it once, got a poor result and gave up. A little structured training fixes all three.

**[OPTIONAL PERSONAL EXAMPLE: one sentence about a time you, or someone on your team, tried a new tool once, got a poor result and gave up on it.]**

### 2. Address the fear first · 0:20

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Honesty builds more trust than reassurance"]**

Before you teach any tools, be direct about your intentions. If it's true, say something like: "We're using AI to take away the work nobody enjoys and to serve customers better. This isn't about replacing anyone."

If roles will change, say how, and how you'll support people through it. Honesty builds more trust than reassurance.

Then have honest answers ready for the concerns you'll hear. Take "Will this replace me?" Share your intent, show which tasks you're targeting, and explain how freed-up time will be used.

"It makes things up." It can, and that's exactly why we review and verify, and you'll learn how.

"I'm not technical." If you can write a clear email, you can do this; it's delegation, not coding.

"It feels like cheating." Like a template or a calculator, it's a tool, and you're still accountable for the result.

And "Customers will hate it." Customers see what we send, and a person reviews everything.

### 3. The four-session training plan · 1:30

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "1. Foundations · 2. The business brief · 3. Your workflows · 4. Show and tell"]**

The training plan is four sessions, about a week apart, using your team's real work rather than toy examples. Session one covers foundations: what AI is good and bad at, the usage policy, the traffic-light rules and first prompts.

Session two is the business brief and a tour of your prompt library. Session three is ninety minutes, hands-on, in pairs, with your actual pilot projects. Each session ends with homework, like using AI for three real tasks or timing a new workflow for a week.

Session four is show and tell. Everyone shares one win and one failure. The failures are as valuable as the wins, because they show you where prompts or reference documents need work, and they make it safe to admit when something didn't go well.

### 4. Appoint AI champions · 2:25

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Champions: curious · respected · protected time"]**

Next, choose one or two AI champions. They should be curious and respected by their colleagues, and they don't need to be the most technical.

They're the first port of call for questions. They maintain the prompt library, run a short monthly show-and-tell, and collect wins, problems and ideas to pass on to you.

Here's the mistake to avoid: champions without protected time. Give them a couple of hours each week, because volunteer enthusiasm fades quickly without it.

### 5. Practice with safe exercises · 3:00

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Realistic, but fictional"]**

Practice works best with realistic but fictional material, so nobody is tempted to paste real customer data while learning. AI can write those exercises for you.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
I'm training my team to use AI. Create five practice exercises for a [ROLE] at a [BUSINESS TYPE], based on tasks they really do: [LIST TASKS].
For each exercise, give: the scenario, fictional sample input they can use (no real customer data), what a good result looks like, and one common mistake to watch for.
~~~

You should get a scenario, fictional sample input, what a good result looks like and one common mistake to watch for, for every exercise. That's a ready-made practice session.

### 6. Make sharing a habit, and measure it · 3:35

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Which workflows did you use? · How much time did they save? · What went wrong?"]**

To keep it going, set up a shared channel for AI tips, prompts and results. Spend ten minutes in a regular team meeting on one win and one lesson learned, and celebrate time saved and how it was reinvested. Add AI basics and the usage policy to onboarding for every new hire.

To measure adoption, track a few simple signals: how many people use AI for work each week, which library prompts get used, self-reported time saved and quality issues found in review.

A three-question monthly survey is enough: which AI workflows did you use this month, roughly how much time did they save you each week, and what's one thing that frustrated you or went wrong?

### 7. Do this now · 4:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Schedule session 1 · Choose your champions · Tell your team"]**

Here's your exercise. Schedule session one and choose your champions.

Then tell your team what's happening and why. You can draft the announcement with AI.

**[SCREEN RECORDING]** Paste this prompt:

~~~
Help me write a short, honest message to my team of [NUMBER] announcing that we're starting to use AI in [AREAS]. Explain why, what will change, what won't, how we'll train everyone, and that I want their ideas and concerns. Tone: [TONE]. Under 200 words. Don't overpromise.
~~~

Now edit it until it sounds like you. This message is coming from you, not from a tool.

### 8. Recap and what's next · 5:00

**[ON CAMERA]**

Let's recap. Adoption is a people challenge, so address fears honestly before teaching tools. Four short sessions on real work beat one long demo.

Champions with protected time keep momentum going after training ends. And share wins and failures openly, and measure adoption with a few simple questions.

Once your team is using AI, you'll want to know what it's actually worth. In the next lesson, Measure ROI: Baseline, Measure, Iterate, you'll build a simple calculator that shows which projects are paying off.
