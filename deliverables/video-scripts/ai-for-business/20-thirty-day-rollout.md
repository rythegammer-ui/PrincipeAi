### 2. Weeks 1 and 2: foundations and a quick win · 0:30

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Week 1: Foundations · Week 2: The quick win goes live"]**

Week one is foundations. Finish your audit and confirm your first three projects, measure the baseline for all three, approve your tools and set up business accounts. Then publish version one of your usage policy, write your context pack and voice guide, and run training session one.

The milestone: everyone knows the rules and has used AI for at least one real task.

In week two, the quick win goes live. Build its prompts and test them on ten real examples, write a one-page SOP with the prompt and the review rule, then run training session two and launch. Start your prompt library with five entries, and the milestone is that the quick win is part of someone's daily routine.

### 3. Weeks 3 and 4: launch, measure, decide · 1:25

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Week 3: Core workflow and growth bet launch · Week 4: Measure and decide"]**

In week three, you launch the core workflow, with full human review, and your growth bet, like your follow-up cadence or content engine. Training session three is hands-on with the new workflows, and your champions collect issues daily so prompts get fixed quickly. The milestone: all three projects are running.

Week four is measure and decide. Measure all three projects against their baselines, run the show-and-tell session, and decide for each one: standardize, improve or stop.

Update your SOPs, prompt library and policy with what you learned, and choose the next two or three opportunities from your audit. The milestone is a completed AI scorecard and a plan for the next 30 days.

### 5. Why rollouts stall · 2:40

**[ON CAMERA]**

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Tie AI to real workflows · Standardize prompts · Protect time"]**

Most stalls have simple fixes. If nobody's using it, tie AI to specific workflows, make it the default in your SOPs and lean on your champions. If results are inconsistent, add better examples and reference documents, and standardize the prompts.

If you don't have time, shrink the scope, protect two hours a week and lead with the quick win. If something went wrong, review it without blame and adjust the review rule or policy. And if you have too many tools, pick one primary assistant.

### 6. Days 31 to 90, and when to get help · 3:15

**[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Standardize · Connect · Re-audit quarterly · Re-test prompts"]**

After the first month, standardize the winners. Once a workflow is stable and proven, consider connecting the steps with your CRM's automation or a no-code tool. Repeat the opportunity audit every quarter, and re-test your most-used prompts whenever your AI tools update.

Everything in this course can be done in-house. Outside help starts to make sense when your workflows span several systems, you want a customer-facing assistant built on your knowledge base with proper testing and escalation, you handle sensitive or regulated data, or you've proven the value but don't have the time to scale it.

The written lesson has a checklist for choosing a partner. Look for one that starts with your workflows, not their favorite tool, builds in human review, explains how your data is handled and measures results against a baseline. Choose one that leaves you more capable, not more dependent.
